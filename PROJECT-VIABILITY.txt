PROJECT VIABILITY — working assessment, 2026-11-21

Can AnSci become a useful chatbot by combining reusable mechanisms, knowledge graphs, and existing language resources? It is a worthwhile engineering and research question. The present evidence supports continued development toward a measured range of conversation; it does not yet establish how broad or fluent that range can become.

WHAT HAS BEEN MEASURED

The project contains usable components and language resources. Their vocabulary and coverage counts describe what those resources contain; they do not tell us how often the complete chatbot understands an ordinary sentence or responds appropriately. SymSpell, parser components, ontology operations, and other modules have been evaluated within particular scopes.

The sealed Link Grammar parser set contains 21 deliberately selected cases. In the recorded run, graph checks passed F1 5/5, F2 3/4, F3 1/4, F4 0/4, and F5 1/4. These checks ask whether specified graph content survives. They are useful for locating failures, but they are not a representative sample of everyday chat, an independent evaluation, or a measure of overall conversational success. See docs/ansci/root-copula-controls.md and data/ansci/linkage-heldout-v1.json.

Link Grammar's solver and shadow were integrated, but the reported routing work did not demonstrate a graph improvement: F1 routing had no observed firings and the attempted F2 routing was reverted after its gate failed. The subsequent F2 increase from 2/4 to 3/4 came from separate parser changes for a copular predicate and a subject noun phrase. The coat/root-selection case remains unresolved.

The system-map audit found examples of built capabilities that the normal chat path does not consume, as well as planned components that are incomplete. A component's tests or store size therefore cannot by themselves establish that its capability is available in a conversation. The audit and its scope are recorded in docs/ansci/system-map-audit-2026-11-20.md.

WHAT REMAINS UNKNOWN

We do not know what fraction of ordinary sentences AnSci parses correctly or what fraction of ordinary conversations it handles successfully. A parse can be produced yet omit or misattribute meaning; a correct graph can also lead to an unsuitable reply. A representative end-to-end evaluation is needed to distinguish those outcomes and determine their frequency.

The tests above were authored within the project. A sealed set helps detect tuning to specific cases, but independent authorship and review would provide additional evidence. A small independently authored dialogue sample can reveal failure types; any numerical estimate must be described within the limits of its size and how it was selected.

REASONS FOR CAUTION

A useful conversation requires several things to work together: interpreting language, tracking what each person said, using relevant knowledge, handling uncertainty, and expressing a response. Weakness at any point can affect the turn. For illustration, five independent stages each succeeding 90% of the time would all succeed together about 59% of the time. This is not a measurement of AnSci: its stages may have different rates and their failures may be correlated.

Fragments and references such as “Tomorrow instead” or “the blue one” depend on conversational context. Ambiguity can also require knowledge beyond grammar. Generating natural, appropriately timed language and repairing misunderstandings are separate challenges that require direct evaluation.

Earlier symbolic systems such as SHRDLU, Cyc, and OpenCog illustrate the difficulty of expanding structured reasoning and language capabilities across domains. Their histories are useful warnings about integration, knowledge acquisition, and generalization; they do not prove a ceiling for AnSci or determine what its design will achieve.

THE OPPORTUNITY TO INVESTIGATE

Reusable general operations, explicit knowledge, and traces may make gaps easier to locate and improvements applicable across many situations. Wuxing and the proposed Hexagram structures may provide useful organizing or steering mechanisms if their effects can be identified and measured. Whether these mechanisms improve conversation, and whether their interaction produces useful emergent behavior, are hypotheses rather than established results. Compare enabled and disabled versions on cases selected independently of the implementation, while checking that any effect reaches the actual chat path.

CURRENT DIRECTION

Develop a dependable chatbot in a measured range of use, while retaining the longer-term goal of broader language and emergent behavior. Use traces to classify failures before changing code: missing module, missing knowledge, incorrect interpretation, faulty reasoning, reply-generation problem, or input the system cannot yet handle. Prefer general mechanisms and evaluate their effects across cases.

Next, author an independent sample of ordinary dialogue, record expected meaning and acceptable responses, and run it through the live chat path. Use the results to choose the next integration or parser improvement and track regressions. The near-term gaps and sequencing are recorded in ROADMAP-EMERGENT-PROPERTIES.md.
