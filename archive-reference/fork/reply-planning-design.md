# Reply Planning from the Ontology — design note

Status: DESIGN NOTE (2026-11-15). Commissioned by the user as a
short note for judgment before anything is built. Nothing in
this note is implemented. It covers parts 1 and 2 of the Path to
a Working Chatbot (dialogue core; reply generation). The user's
decisions on §7 questions 1, 3 and 4 were recorded the same day
(§7); question 2 remains open. EXPANDED 2026-11-16 (§§9–12) at
the user's direction, from the fork chat's pre-build review:
the decisions the build needs before it starts, the complete
build programme with acceptance and gates per slice, and the
definition of done for a usable chat on the dashboard. With
§§9–12 in place the programme is specified to be run end to
end; the one user decision it still needs is §7.2, for which
§11 states a default.

## 1. The gap

The turn loop (`src/fork/turn.ts`) already does three of the
four things a conversation needs. It parses a sentence into a
meaning graph; it retains the semantics in a session (entities,
events, states, carried across turns); and it derives
conclusions — derivation paths over the correspondence graph,
cycle paths between the turn's states and the session's. What
it does not do is speak from those conclusions. Its replies are
a small hand-written wording set, steered by the mechanism but
not generated by it. The generator (`src/fork/generate.ts`) can
turn any meaning graph back into English or Chinese — measured
at 97.7–98.2% feature preservation on round trips — but nothing
builds it a *reply* graph. It grades round-trips; it is not the
reply surface.

The missing step is reply planning: take the turn's
conclusions, build a meaning graph for the reply, hand that
graph to the generator. The naive way to build it — a planner
module with a dialogue-act taxonomy and template assembly —
would break two standing rules at once: the graph-distance rule
(a reply is never a direct graph query pasted out; every turn
travels through the whole architecture) and Part F (try the
ontology first; an engineering fallback is reported as an
ontology failure). So the planning step itself has to be made
out of the ontology. This note is how.

**Scope of this stage.** The planner's inputs are the turn's
derivations and the session's situation record — and the
record's limits are the planner's limits. Prior conversation
enters only as that record: the entities, events and states the
session has retained, in distilled form. There is no intent arc
yet — the accumulating picture of what the conversation is
about, named as outstanding work in Path part 1 — and no use of
raw transcript history beyond what the record kept. Knowledge
of the situation is the same record: what has been
established, what the current states are, who is speaking. It
carries nothing wider — no time, place or circumstance; the
fork's session has none. Knowledge of the person does not
enter at all: the fork models no person. The speaker is
recorded as "you", addressed in the second person, and nothing
about them — history, preferences, what they are like — is
retained or used. Disposition (§7.4) is not person knowledge;
it is setup, the same for whoever is speaking. Widening any of
these inputs is later work, and the planner's interface should
not be built so as to preclude it.

## 2. Three operations, all already prototyped somewhere

### 2.1 What to say: the move is a cycle relation

The steering probe already selects one move per turn, and its
selection is not a dialogue act — it is whichever relation
holds in the graph. The new state's element generates toward
the next content: follow it, toward expression. A state
controls an earlier state: address the restrainer, to release
what it restrains. A state has been vented: meet it from the
restraining side. The probe makes that one choice and hands it
to hand-written wording. The design generalises the same
selection to composing the whole reply: candidate contents are
the turn's derivations, and where rivals compete, the
arbitration is the sense machinery's — coherence support from
the surrounding graph (the pattern that already chooses between
rival readings of a word, in `src/fork/sense.ts`). No act
taxonomy is introduced. If a conversational move has no cycle
relation or derivation behind it, the planner does not invent
one; see §4.

### 2.2 How strongly to say it: support becomes qualification

Derivations already carry priced support — the sense layer's
weights: same element 1.0, a generating relation 0.8, a
controlling relation 0.4. Under this design, the support of the
derivation a reply rests on is written into the reply graph
itself, as the qualification states the generator already knows
how to realise — the modality and aspect states of the stage-5
machinery. A strongly supported conclusion is built as a plain
predication. A weakly supported one is built qualified, so the
hedge is in the meaning graph, checkable in the trace, rather
than pasted into the wording. And when no derivation reaches
the floor, the computation's output is the decline — which the
turn loop already produces, naming what is missing. Abstention
stops being a policy layered over the planner and becomes a
result of the same arithmetic as everything else.

### 2.3 Building the reply graph: a graph-to-graph transform

A derivation path is already a chain of typed relations. The
winter derivation — winter, ELEMENT_OF Water, POLARITY_OF Yin,
HAS_QUALITY cold — becomes a meaning graph mechanically: the
topic entity as subject, the derived quality as its predicated
state, and the path itself available as subordinate causal
material. That is the shape of the probe's one fully-derived
answer today ("Winter is cold, because it is the season turned
most fully toward cold") — except that today the sentence is
hand-worded, and under this design it is generated, from a
graph the planner built out of the derivation. The reply graph
is an ordinary meaning graph of the parser's own record type,
so the checker, the feature machinery and the round-trip
measurements all apply to replies unchanged.

Questions are the same transform run on a gap. A genuine gap —
the only kind the architecture permits a question about — is a
graph fact, in one of two shapes: a role the verb's valency
requires that the situation does not fill (the stage-1 valency
machinery already computes these), or a node whose
classification is split between two rival derivations with
comparable support (finite ambiguity, in the sealed protocol's
sense). Asking is generating a question graph whose missing
piece is exactly that gap. A question with no gap behind it is
not asked; a gap the person cannot fill is not asked about
either — both halves of the standing rule fall out of the
representation.

## 3. Worked shapes

- **Winter.** Asked why winter is cold, the planner takes the
  derivation path, builds {winter — predicated state: cold}
  with the path as its causal subordinate, at plain-assertion
  strength (the path is short and same-direction). The
  generator realises it. The trace shows the path, the support,
  and the graph — the same three things the probe's trace shows
  today, with the graph now the reply's actual source.
- **Anger, three turns.** "I heard the news" records an event
  and no state; with nothing for the cycles to steer by, the
  reply takes up the event and asks after its content — the gap
  is the event's unfilled content role. "I was furious" adds a
  Wood state; the generating direction selects expression, and
  the reply graph predicates the person's fury against the
  recorded event and asks for its venting. "I shouted at him"
  vents it; the controlling side selects settling. The probe
  makes these three choices today; the design changes only
  where the words come from.

## 4. What stays engineering — and what gets recorded

Word order and surface realisation stay in the connective
layer, where the two-layer design already puts them: generation
is meaning graph plus language precedence templates, and the
templates carry no knowledge claims. The ontology decides the
content, the stance and the strength; it does not conjugate
verbs.

Social formulae — greetings, leave-takings, pure politeness —
have no derivation behind them and make no knowledge claim.
They are the likeliest place for the build to reach for a
template. Under Part F that reach is recorded as an ontology
gap in the build report, named as one, rather than passing as
a quiet exception; whether a thin conventional layer is
admitted for them, labelled as conventional, is the user's
call at that point, not the build's.

## 5. Pieces: what exists, what is new

- Exists: `turn.ts` (the probe — v0 of move selection, one
  choice per turn, hand wording); `session.ts` (the situation
  record the planner reads); `join.ts` `derive()` (derivation
  paths); `sense.ts` (support weights and the arbitration
  pattern); `generate.ts` (realisation, both languages);
  the stage-5 node states (qualification the generator can
  already realise).
- New: one transform — situation plus derivations in, reply
  meaning graph out (working name `plan.ts`). No new node or
  edge vocabulary is proposed. If the build finds the reply
  graph needs a relation the meaning graph does not have, that
  is a finding to record and bring back, not a silent
  extension.

## 6. Acceptance

- Fresh transcripts are the gate, as they already are for the
  turn loop: new scenarios, judged turn by turn, never the ones
  that exposed a failure.
- Every reply's trace names the derivation it rests on, its
  support, and the reply graph built from it. A reply whose
  trace cannot name those three does not count, however good it
  reads.
- The surface ban extends to generated replies unchanged: no
  element, polarity or cycle vocabulary on the surface (the
  existing leak scan in `test/fork-turn.test.ts` runs over the
  new surface too).
- A decline must cite its ground: the support shortfall, or the
  missing role. "I don't know" without a named ground is a
  planner failure, recorded as one.
- The four scripted scenarios keep passing. Their traces keep
  the same structure; only the wording's origin changes.

## 7. Decisions (user, 2026-11-15) and the one open question

1. **Composition order — decided: build both, support is the
   default.** When several conclusions compose into one reply,
   both orderings are built out so they can be seen side by
   side: ordered by support, and ordered by relation to the
   newest state. Support-order is the default; relation-order
   is the comparison that can displace it on evidence.
2. **The causal subordinate — OPEN.** When the planner builds a
   reply from a derivation, the path can be realised as a
   subordinate "because" clause, or left in the trace. Two
   policies are on the table. (a) Demand-driven: the path is
   verbalised only when the person asks why, or challenges the
   conclusion; otherwise the reply is the bare conclusion.
   (b) Support-driven: the path is verbalised whenever the
   derivation's support is middling — the reply offers its own
   evidence exactly when the conclusion is not strong enough
   to stand naked; strong conclusions stand alone. Under (b),
   §2.2's dial turns one notch further: support controls not
   only qualification but evidence-offering. A "why" question
   triggers the clause under either policy. Undecided.
3. **Language — decided: English-first, transform
   language-neutral.** The planner lands English-first (the
   turn loop's current reach); the transform itself is built
   language-neutral, so Chinese reply graphs follow from the
   same machinery when the turn loop's Chinese side is built,
   rather than the planner being rebuilt for them.
4. **Disposition — decided, broader than this note's
   proposal.** The note proposed disposition as a tie-break
   bias at §2.1's arbitration and nowhere else. The user's
   ruling supersedes that: disposition is itself composed of a
   Five Element combination, and it influences the system's
   processes generally — arbitration, steering, generation —
   so that a disposition of one composition actually produces
   different responses from a disposition of another, by
   changing how the processes run, not by supplying content.
   The same composition treatment is available to other
   architectural aspects: an aspect can be parameterised by an
   elemental composition and thereby change behaviour across
   the system. Two standing limits hold unchanged: disposition
   is architecture, never surface (no reply names it), and it
   biases processing only — it is never a source of testimony.

## 8. The supportive structure, designed the same way

Reply planning is one piece. The prior architecture
(`docs/design-conversation-engine.md` — pieces A–G plus a
Context module, the saying register S1–S16, the morphs)
explored the whole supportive structure a conversation needs,
in conventional terms: a module per aspect. Its own governing
economy was graph-first — modules hold questions and
judgements, the graph holds the knowledge — and the fork
applies the same economy with Wuxing as the graph. For each
aspect: ask what the ontology already does that supplies it.
The module shrinks to a question the graph answers, or retires.
The point is composition: pieces designed as operations over
one representation — graphs, derivations, support — fit
together at every joint, where a template architecture's
pieces fit only at their seams, and read as templates.

1. **Awareness (A).** Prior form: a situated self computed per
   turn — place, time, season, daypart — plus the person, plus
   the role. Ours: the frame is classified, not read off a
   clock. Season carries its attested element (the Yueling
   correspondences the classification work already uses),
   daypart its polarity; awareness becomes more classified
   content in the situation record, able to steer like any
   other classified content. The person is absent at this stage
   (§1 scope). The role — who the chat is in this conversation
   — is an elemental composition, under the same treatment as
   disposition (§7.4): a parameter set, not a script.
2. **Discourse breakdown (B).** Prior form: a breakdown graph
   with a contents submodule — people, places, things, times,
   events — categorisation defeasible, rivals kept. Ours: the
   breakdown is the meaning graph. The contents kinds are its
   node and role classes (entities, events, time attachments,
   locations), and categorisation is the parser's own
   classification act, defeasible exactly as sense choice is:
   rivals kept, chosen by coherence, visible in the trace.
   This piece already exists — it is the parser and the sense
   machinery, seen from the conversation's side.
3. **Intent (C).** Prior form: an objective classification —
   inform, ask, correct, request, schmooze — with person
   knowledge consulted first. Ours: intent is read off the
   graph's shape and its effect on the session. Mood and
   construction say what kind of turn it is (a question graph
   asks; a predication informs; a correction supersedes a
   session state), and what the turn's content does to the
   standing states says the rest — a new state that restrains a
   standing one is not mere informing. No separate intent
   taxonomy is kept. The schmooze class — a turn whose correct
   reply is social, with no graph content — is §4's phatic gap,
   recorded as one.
4. **Memory (D).** Prior form: short-term topics, referents,
   trace ring, corrections; a long-term person file under a
   write discipline. Ours: short-term is the session record
   (exists, thin — §1). Long-term is the banking pipeline (Path
   part 4): what a conversation establishes reaches the durable
   graph only banked, gated, individually reversible — the
   write discipline is the banking gate. The person file is not
   carried over at this stage.
5. **Operative context.** Prior form: a Context module
   assembling topic weights and facets, its pressure acting
   during analysis rather than as a late tiebreak, its assembly
   recorded in the trace. Ours: no separate module. Coherence
   support already acts inside analysis — sense selection is
   the proof — and the generalisation is that derivation
   candidates throughout processing are weighted by the
   session's standing states and topics as they are computed,
   never re-ranked afterwards. The weights ride in the trace,
   as the prior design required of its Context assembly.
6. **Solving (E).** Prior form: triage — retrieve, or construct
   a set under constraints, or both — with the empty set as an
   honest result; an EverydaySense module for typicality.
   Ours: retrieve is derivation over the correspondence graph.
   Solve is derivation under the brakes — a conclusion exists
   only with its registered rule, its price and its path,
   which is Part E's discipline doing the constraint solver's
   honesty work. The empty set is the decline (§2.2).
   Typicality is not a module: it is correspondence support and
   density in the graph itself.
7. **Articulation (F) and the saying register.** Compose and
   articulate are §2 of this note — the planner and the
   generator. The register's items, where they map: S1
   aggregation is composing derivations (§7.1). S3 cohesion:
   the prior design's rule was that connectives are claims;
   ours is its graph form — a connective is realised only when
   its relation exists between the reply graph and the session
   record (continuation: a shared topic entity; consequence: a
   derivation from a prior state; contrast: a controlling
   relation; return: a reactivated topic), and an unearned
   marker is an ungrounded claim, banned on the same ground as
   any other. S4 output reference is the session's recency
   resolution run over the reply graph's own entities. S6
   epistemic forms are §2.2 — support written as qualification
   states. S8 question forms are §2.3 — gap questions, whose
   clarification branches are the rival derivations themselves.
   S12 output repair is state supersession: a correction
   replaces a session state, the reply predicates the corrected
   state, and the acknowledgement's content is the recorded
   delta between the two — not a formula. S13 set presentation
   is coordinated predications over a shared derivation,
   ordered per §7.1. S14 echo is a generation rule: the reply
   graph reuses the person's own nodes where the derivation
   permits — their content taken up, as the turn discipline
   already requires, now as construction rather than wording.
   S16 definition form is a reply graph built from the
   classification record: the concept, its aspect and element,
   its distinguishing correspondence. S5 phatic turns are §4's
   recorded gap. S9 surface grammar belongs to the connective
   layer (§4). S10 register and tone, S11 length: morph
   parameters (item 9 below) — they select among grounded
   behaviours and never license content. S15 delivery is piece
   G, unchanged in kind.
8. **The ValueSystem (S2's prior form).** Prior form: content
   selection and ordering by a value layer drawn from the Book
   of Changes. Ours: the selection and ordering function is
   supplied by cycle relations and support (§2.1, §7.1), and a
   separate value layer is not carried into the fork's design.
   Recorded as a divergence for the user's judgment, not a
   silent drop: if a value layer is wanted here, under the
   telos it would sit in the architecture beside disposition,
   as a composition that biases selection — never on the
   surface, and never a source of content.
9. **Morphs.** The prior design's parameterised pieces return
   as the general form of §7.4: a piece's behaviour is
   parameter-selected by an elemental composition — disposition
   first; role, register, length as the same treatment reaches
   them — computed per conversation and changeable without
   surgery. The prior guardrail carries over whole: an
   invariant core never morphs (attestation, the decline,
   memory's write discipline), and a composition change is
   judged like any other change, by its declared transcript
   effect.
10. **Initiative.** Missing in the prior design; its role
    follow-up policy was only a seed. Ours: the gap machinery
    of §2.3 run on the session instead of the turn — a standing
    state whose generating direction has gone unexpressed
    across turns, a gap that persists — under the same
    discipline: initiative may take up a standing thread or ask
    about a genuine gap, and may never manufacture an opening.
11. **Technology interface (G).** Unchanged in kind: the shells
    (fork:chat, the dashboard's chat box) drive the turn loop
    and hold no conversational logic.

## 9. Pre-build decisions (2026-11-16)

Four things the design above assumes but does not settle.
They are settled here, before any code, because each one is
expensive to retrofit. They come from the fork chat's review
of this note; the user directed that they be written in.

### 9.1 Compositions: one format, a stated scope, an observable

Disposition (§7.4), the role (§8.1) and the morphs (§8.9) are
all elemental compositions. They share ONE format, the one
the bridge-graph design already settled for compositions of
modern terms: a ranked predominance list — predominant,
secondary, trace — never percentages, never a vector of
weights. A `Composition` is data: an ordered list of elements
with at most three ranks. Two compositions are the same when
their ranked lists are the same.

Scope — what a composition may and may not do. A composition
MAY bias: (a) arbitration among grounded reply candidates
(§2.1), by re-weighting support within the bounds of §9.3;
(b) move selection among moves that each have a cycle
relation or derivation behind them; (c) the qualification
thresholds of §9.2, within the declared band of that table;
(d) register and length parameters (§8.7, S10/S11), selecting
among grounded behaviours. A composition MAY NEVER: create
content or a candidate that has no derivation; alter a
classification, a derivation or its price; override the
decline floor (§2.2); or touch the invariant core —
attestation, the decline, memory's write discipline (§8.9).
Disposition biases processing only (§7.4); this paragraph is
that rule made checkable.

Observable — a composition is admitted only with a declared
transcript effect: a named scenario, run under two
compositions, whose traces must differ in a stated way (which
candidate won, which threshold applied, which move was
selected). A composition whose declared effect cannot be seen
in a trace is not a parameter; it is a story about one, and
the build does not admit it. In v1 a session's disposition is
set at session start, recorded in the trace header of every
turn, and changeable only between sessions.

### 9.2 Strength pricing: support is not the whole price

§2.2 prices a reply by its derivation's support. That is
half the price. The other half is the standing of the
classifications the derivation rests on — the tiers the
classification work already keeps distinct (Part E's ladder).
A reply built on a dictionary-derived classification at the
gate must not be spoken with the force of one built on an
attested row. The rule, stated once, as data (a table in the
planner, not conditions scattered through it):

- A derivation's **ceiling** is set by the weakest
  classification standing on its path, by tier: attested
  (fork-curated / grade A) — plain assertion permitted;
  established correspondence (grade B-direct) — plain or
  lightly qualified; derived (B-derived records,
  wordnet-derived climbs, verb-domain classifications) —
  qualified by default; prediction-tier (T4) — spoken only
  as a possibility, never as fact.
- The derivation's **support** (§2.2's weights) then sets the
  strength at or below that ceiling: high support speaks at
  the ceiling; middling support drops one level (plain →
  qualified, qualified → possibility); support below the
  floor is the decline (§2.2), whatever the ceiling.
- The pricing decision — ceiling, support, resulting level —
  is written into the trace for every reply, beside the
  derivation it rests on (§6).

This is the project's standing pricing pattern (a chain is
priced by its weakest leg, as in H7) applied to speech:
unpriced inference is never spoken as fact.

### 9.3 The support weights are adopted consciously, flaw included

§2.1 and §8.5 reuse the sense layer's support weights (same
element 1.0, generating 0.8, controlling 0.4). Those weights
carry a recorded defect: generating support runs in both
directions, so a candidate can claim support from elements it
generates — the mechanism behind the one sealed-set overturn
(the X1 pattern, docs/fork/sense-challenge.md). The anchor
repair disarmed that instance; the scorer flaw itself is not
fixed. The weights are adopted here DELIBERATELY, for the
lower-stakes job of ordering and selecting among grounded
candidates — with one audit hook: whenever an arbitration's
winning margin comes from a generating-support term, the
trace says so, so transcripts can be checked for the X1
pattern rather than trusting that it stayed disarmed.

### 9.4 The session record grows four fields, first

The planner sees history only through the session record
(§1). Four additions are prerequisites, made in the first
build slice, before the planner:

1. Every standing state carries its derivation path, its
   support, and the provenance tier of the classifications on
   that path (§9.2's pricing reads these; initiative, §8.10,
   reads the path).
2. Topics are first-class in the record: a topic is an entity
   with the turn indices at which it was raised and last
   touched (cohesion's "return", §8.7 S3, and initiative read
   these).
3. The record carries the session's disposition composition
   (§9.1) and a turn counter.
4. Supersession is recorded as a delta — the state replaced,
   the state replacing it, the turn — not as a silent
   overwrite (§8.7 S12's acknowledgement is built from this
   delta).

## 10. The build programme

Slices in order. Each slice: its deliverable, its acceptance,
its gates. Standing gates for every slice: the full test
suite green (355 at programme start, plus the slice's new
tests), typecheck clean, the demo exhibit non-regressed, the
four scripted scenarios passing with their trace structure
unchanged, and the surface leak scan (§6) run over every new
reply path. Fresh transcript scenarios for a slice are
authored BEFORE the slice's code first runs against them and
are frozen once written; a slice is judged on its transcripts
plus the standing gates, never on either alone. The build
works in one working copy staged on current main; pushes only
at the user's word for that push, per the standing rule.

- **R0 — Record and pricing foundations.** §9.4's record
  fields; the `Composition` type and a disposition parameter
  on the session (default: a declared neutral composition);
  §9.2's pricing table as data; trace extensions (composition
  header, per-reply pricing record, §9.3's generating-margin
  flag). No reply behaviour changes: the probe's wording still
  speaks. Acceptance: the four scenarios produce identical
  replies with richer traces; the new fields are populated and
  test-pinned.
- **R1 — The transform.** `plan.ts` (§5): derivation →
  reply meaning graph (§2.3), single-conclusion replies,
  strength per §9.2, realised by the existing generator.
  turn.ts speaks from the plan wherever a derivation backs
  the turn; the probe's hand wording remains only where no
  derivation exists, and each such turn is recorded as
  wording-not-derived in the trace. Acceptance: the winter
  derivation is answered from a generated graph end to end;
  anger turns 2–3 are generated; fresh transcript set T-R1
  (authored first) judged turn by turn per §6.
- **R2 — Composition of conclusions.** §7.1 built both ways:
  support order (default) and relation-to-newest-state order,
  the comparison visible in the trace; set presentation
  (§8.7 S13) as coordinated predications over a shared
  derivation. Acceptance: multi-conclusion turns in T-R2
  compose in the declared order; switching the default is a
  data change, and the trace shows both orderings.
- **R3 — Gap questions and grounded declines.** §2.3's two
  gap shapes (unfilled required role; rival derivations at
  comparable support) generate question graphs; clarification
  branches are the rival derivations; every decline cites its
  ground (§6). Acceptance: T-R3 contains a genuine-gap
  question the person can fill, a rival-classification
  clarification, and declines whose grounds are named and
  correct; a question with no gap behind it does not occur.
- **R4 — Disposition live.** The session composition actually
  parameterises arbitration, move selection and the §9.2
  thresholds within §9.1's scope; register/length morphs
  (§8.7 S10/S11) select among grounded behaviours only.
  Acceptance: the declared transcript pairs of §9.1 — at
  minimum a Wood-predominant and a Water-predominant
  composition over the same scenarios — differ in the traces
  in the declared ways, and in no undeclared way (same
  conclusions reached; different selection/qualification).
- **R5 — Cohesion, echo, repair.** §8.7's S3/S4/S12/S14 as
  construction rules over the reply graph: a connective only
  where its relation exists; echo reusing the person's own
  nodes where the derivation permits; correction handled as
  supersession, the reply's acknowledgement built from the
  recorded delta. Acceptance: T-R5 contains a topic return
  with its connective earned by the record, an echo that
  reuses the person's content, and a correction whose
  acknowledgement names the delta — each checkable in the
  trace.
- **R6 — Initiative and the awareness frame.** §8.10's
  session-level gap scan (a standing state whose generating
  direction has gone unexpressed; a persisting topic gap),
  bounded: at most one initiative move in any five turns, and
  never when the person's turn presents a gap or question of
  its own. §8.1's frame: season and daypart classified into
  the record from the session's clock (the shell supplies
  date and time; classification by the attested season rows
  and daypart polarity), able to steer as classified content.
  Acceptance: T-R6 shows one initiative move taken from a
  standing thread with its gap named in the trace, and no
  initiative where the discipline forbids it.
- **R7 — Dashboard integration.** The dashboard's chat box
  (already at the top of the page) runs the full planner;
  every reply's trace expandable to the derivation, support,
  pricing record and composition behind it; a disposition
  switcher offering the programme's named compositions,
  labelled as an architecture demonstration. The planner's
  graphs are language-neutral (§7.3); the turn loop's Chinese
  side is NOT part of this programme (§12). Acceptance: the
  §11 behaviours are exercised through the dashboard itself,
  in the browser, with traces inspected — not only through
  the test harness.

## 11. Definition of done — a usable chat on the dashboard

The programme is complete when the dashboard chat does all
of the following, each checkable in a transcript with its
trace — a behaviour that reads well but whose trace cannot
name its derivation, support and pricing does not count (§6):

1. **Converse over multiple turns on the graph's home
   ground** — emotions and states, seasons and weather,
   the body, food and the everyday objects the classification
   work covers — retaining what was established, resolving
   reference against the record, and taking up the person's
   own content (echo as construction, R5).
2. **Answer from derivations, and show its working on
   demand**: conclusions spoken at the strength their pricing
   earns (§9.2); a "why" question answered with the derivation
   path as its causal subordinate. §7.2 (when the path is
   verbalised unprompted) is the one decision this programme
   still needs from the user; until it is decided, the
   default is policy (a), demand-driven — the path is spoken
   when asked for, or when the conclusion is challenged —
   and the default is recorded here so the run is not blocked
   on it.
3. **Ask only genuine questions**: follow-ups arise from
   valency gaps and rival derivations (R3), and initiative
   from session-level gaps under its bound (R6) — never from
   a schedule of things to ask.
4. **Handle correction as supersession**: the record's delta
   is acknowledged from the record, and later replies use the
   corrected state (R5).
5. **Decline with a named ground** outside its knowledge —
   the missing role, the support shortfall, the classification
   the graph does not hold — and stay in the conversation
   afterwards (a decline is a turn, not an ending).
6. **Vary with disposition, demonstrably**: the same
   conversation under two named compositions produces the
   declared trace differences (R4), with no difference in
   what is concluded — disposition changes how the system
   proceeds, never what it holds to be so.
7. **Keep the surface clean**: no element, polarity, cycle or
   disposition vocabulary in any reply, under the leak scan,
   across all transcript sets.

The honest bounds, stated so "usable" is not overread. The
chat's range is the graph's range: 164 classifications plus
what the climb and the banked stores derive. Outside that
range it will decline or speak thinly, by design — fluency
is not faked beyond the knowledge. Social formulae are §4's
recorded gap: if the transcripts show the conversation
stalling for want of a greeting, that is brought back as a
finding for the user's call, not quietly templated. There is
no person model and no intent arc (§1 scope); the system
knows the conversation, not the person. It is English-first
(§7.3). Within those bounds, "very conversational" has a
specific meaning here: turns arise from the mechanism —
understanding, retention, derivation, priced speech,
grounded questions — and every one of them can be inspected.

## 12. What this programme does not do

Named so the run's scope is exact: the person model and the
intent arc (Path part 1's later work — the planner's
interface must not preclude them, §1); the turn loop's
Chinese side (the planner is language-neutral, §7.3; the
Chinese session is separate work); the feeders and banking
of Path part 4 beyond the write discipline §8.4 already
assumes; the gardener (Path part 6); and any widening of
knowledge coverage, which proceeds on its own track through
the ingestion pipeline and changes what this chat can talk
about without changing how it talks.
