## Why this set exists

Stage 5's family accounting (docs/ansci/stage-5-report.md) found the
construction family at **50.0% on 2 atoms** in the frozen holdout —
under-tested, reporting as neither pass nor fail. The stage-3
construction set (docs/ansci/construction-challenge.md) scores
construction *recognition* on its own ledgers; it does not feed the
family accounting. This set feeds it.

A construction atom is emitted by features.ts per predicate whose
record carries `states.construction`. The parser sets that state in
exactly eight branches — en: imperative, existential, weather;
zh: ba, bei, correlative, a-not-a, serial. The set is built to those
branches' coded signatures, with vocabulary attested in the lexicons.
Copular questions, contractions, fragments and the rest of the stage-3
territory earn no construction atoms and are not re-tested here.

## Located findings

- **bei with an unattested verb throws a TypeError, not a parse
  error** (probe: 猫被狗咬了）. The branch dereferences the missing
  lexicon entry unchecked. The sentence is outside the lexicon's
  territory, but the failure mode is a crash where the parser
  elsewhere fails cleanly.

## Notes

- Adjacent-family losses observed on core items (generation dropping
  patients, goals, a locative suffix, question force, and the
  existential numeral phrases — "There is two.", "There was two.") are
  recorded in the set's notes column and the per-item generations
  above. They score in their own families, not this one, and are not
  chased here.
- The construction-state machinery is language-partitioned: the three
  EN branches and the five ZH branches share no value. There is no
  bilingual construction in the family as coded.
- The set's items run in file order in one process, as the holdout
  accounting runs its corpus; the instrument's warm-state dependence
  (±0.5pt on pooled preservation) applies here too — and in the
  imperative's case the state dependence is the finding itself.
