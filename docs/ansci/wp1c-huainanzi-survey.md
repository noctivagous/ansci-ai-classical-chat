# WP1c — The *Huainanzi* surveyed against the project's tables

Survey commissioned by the user 2026-11-15, after the corpus-access
note: any ancient Chinese text is in reach, and web research on how
and which texts to use is in bounds. The *Huainanzi* (淮南子,
Huáinánzǐ — the court compendium of Liu An, presented 139 BCE) was
named as the first target because it is the most systematically
correspondence-minded text of the early Han. This document records
what it says, chapter by chapter, against the tables the project
already holds (WP1 catalogue §§1–2; the seed and fork-curated
classifications). **Nothing in this survey is banked.** Every item
below is a sourced finding with its verification status; what to do
with the findings is a separate decision.

In one paragraph: the book confirms the project's core tables more
often than it disturbs them — directions, colours, flavours, tones,
numbers, and the five planets all agree with the *Suwen* ch. 4
scheme, and the planets thereby gain a second, earlier attestation.
It adds whole correspondence rows the project does not have
(thearchs and their assistants, heavenly-stem days, sacrificial
organs organised by the controlling cycle, creature genealogies).
It conflicts in specific, recordable places: its seasonal animals
are partly transposed against *Suwen* ch. 4, its body chapter gives
an organ–orifice scheme incompatible with both *Neijing* chapters,
and its geography chapter gives the centre's organ as the stomach,
not the spleen. And on three questions the project has open — the
scope of the "naked" creature class, phase assignments for weather,
and phases for times of day — it gives evidence that cuts in one
direction: weather and the day's hours are handled on the yin-yang
axis with no phase assignments at all, and the naked class turns
out, in every parallel scheme, to be the human class.

## 0. Method and sources

Chapters surveyed in depth, chosen by orientation research
(scholarly literature and the text's own structure agree that chs.
3–5 are the cosmological trilogy and ch. 7 the body chapter):

- Ch. 3 *Tianwen* 天文訓 — full text fetched (Wikisource, received
  text), read entire.
- Ch. 5 *Shize* 時則訓 — full text fetched (Wikisource, Sibu
  Congkan edition, with Gao You's 高誘 commentary interleaved),
  read entire.
- Ch. 4 *Dixing* 地形訓 (also written 墬形訓) — full text fetched
  (a punctuated modern edition, corroborated at the creature
  passages by a second edition), read entire.
- Ch. 7 *Jingshen* 精神訓 — the correspondence passage verified
  through three independent quotations that agree (a Gujin Tushu
  Jicheng page image transcription, a modern punctuated edition,
  and a scholarly article's quotation); read at passage level, not
  as a full-chapter fetch. This is the survey's one method limit
  and is flagged where its evidence is used.

Parallel schemes quoted for the creature question come from the
*Liji · Yueling* commentary tradition (Zheng Xuan 鄭玄, Gao You,
Chen Hao 陳澔 as transmitted) and *Da Dai Liji · Zengzi Tianyuan*
大戴禮記·曾子天圓, both reached through quotation in the sources
above.

Verification discipline follows WP1b: a claim is VERIFIED when the
fetched text was read in place; edition variants and OCR-damaged
passages are flagged where they occur and never silently repaired.
Two passages in the fetched *Dixing* are OCR-damaged (the
human-generation chain's first link and parts of the damp-generation
chain); the damage is noted at the point of use and no load-bearing
claim rests on the damaged characters.

The remaining seventeen chapters contain no systematic

four-chapter survey, not a line-by-line audit of all twenty-one.

## 2. *Tianwen* (ch. 3)

### 2.1 The five-direction table

The chapter poses the question 何謂五星 — "what are the five
stars?" — and answers it with the whole directional scheme
(VERIFIED, received text; corroborated across three further
editions):

> 東方，木也，其帝太皞，其佐句芒，執規而治春。其神為歲星，
> 其獸蒼龍，其音角，其日甲乙。南方，火也，其帝炎帝，其佐朱明，
> 執衡而治夏。其神為熒惑，其獸朱鳥，其音徵，其日丙丁。中央，
> 土也，其帝黃帝，其佐后土，執繩而制四方。其神為鎮星，其獸
> 黃龍，其音宮，其日戊己。西方，金也，其帝少昊，其佐蓐收，
> 執矩而治秋。其神為太白，其獸白虎，其音商，其日庚辛。北方，
> 水也，其帝顓頊，其佐玄冥，執權而治冬。其神為辰星，其獸
> 玄武，其音羽，其日壬癸。

| Row | East | South | Centre | West | North |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Phase | 木 Wood | 火 Fire | 土 Earth | 金 Metal | 水 Water |
| Thearch （帝） | 太皞 | 炎帝 | 黃帝 | 少昊 | 顓頊 |
| Assistant （佐） | 句芒 | 朱明 | 后土 | 蓐收 | 玄冥 |
| Implement | 規 compass | 衡 balance | 繩 plumb-line | 矩 square | 權 weight |
| Governance | 治春 spring | 治夏 summer | 制四方 the four quarters | 治秋 autumn | 治冬 winter |
| Planet （神） | 歲星 Jupiter | 熒惑 Mars | 鎮星 Saturn | 太白 Venus | 辰星 Mercury |
| Creature （獸） | 蒼龍 azure dragon | 朱鳥 vermilion bird | 黃龍 yellow dragon | 白虎 white tiger | 玄武 dark warrior |
| Tone （音） | 角 | 徵 | 宮 | 商 | 羽 |
| Stem-days （日） | 甲乙 | 丙丁 | 戊己 | 庚辛 | 壬癸 |

**Against the project's tables:**

- **Planets AGREE with *Suwen* ch. 4, exactly.** WP1 §1.4 recorded
  the five planets as attested in ch. 4 while the seed tagged them
  `fork-creative`, flagged as provenance-upgrade candidates. They
  are now attested in a second, earlier-dated text (139 BCE).
  Recommendation R1 (§8): upgrade the five planet rows to attested,
  citing both sources.
- **Tones AGREE** with both *Suwen* chapters.
- **New rows the project does not hold:** thearchs, assistants,
  implements, the directional creatures （蒼龍/朱鳥/黃龍/白虎/玄武
  — the four palace creatures plus the yellow dragon), and
  stem-days. None conflicts with anything in the tables; they are
  pure additions, at attested grade, if the project wants them.
- **The centre governs the four quarters and is given no season**
  in this passage （制四方）. For the centre's seasonal placement
  the book elsewhere offers two further arrangements (§2.3, §3.3);
  the catalogue now holds four source-scoped Earth placements in
  total and none should be merged into another.

### 2.2 The cycles, stated

The generating cycle is stated as a chain (VERIFIED):

> 水生木，木生火，火生土，土生金，金生水。

The controlling cycle is **not** chained in this chapter; it
appears as named pairwise relations in a prognostication passage
（子生母曰義，母生子曰保，子母相得曰專，母勝子曰制，子勝母
曰困 — "the child generating the mother is called *yi*; the
mother generating the child, *bao*; mother and child in accord,
*zhuan*; the mother overcoming the child, *zhi*; the child
overcoming the mother, *kun*"). The chained controlling statement
is in *Dixing* (§4.1). Nothing in either chapter adds, removes, or
relabels a cycle edge relative to the project's two cycles.

### 2.3 Stems, branches, and the year's five reigns

- Stems and branches assigned (VERIFIED): 甲乙寅卯，木也；丙丁
  巳午，火也；戊己四季，土也；庚辛申酉，金也；壬癸亥子，水也。
  Note as written: the four earth branches （辰戌丑未） are not
  named; Earth takes the stems 戊己 and **四季 — the ends of the
  four seasons**. This is a third Earth-season arrangement,
  distinct from *Suwen* ch. 4's 长夏 (late summer) and from the
  *Yueling/Shize* centre month (§3.3).
- Life-stage triads (VERIFIED): 木生於亥，壯於卯，死於未 —
  each phase is "born" at one branch, "flourishes" at another,
  "dies" at a third （木 亥/卯/未； 火 寅/午/戌； 土 午/戌/寅；
  金 巳/酉/丑； 水 申/子/辰）. A dimension the project does not
  hold; recorded, not evaluated.
- Five reigns (VERIFIED): in the calendrical passage, each phase
  用事 "holds office" for seventy-two days in generating order
  （木→火→土→金→水）, five reigns making the 360-day round year.
  A fourth Earth placement: Earth reigns in sequence like the
  others, between Fire and Metal.

### 2.4 The sun's stations — GAP-3 evidence

The chapter names fifteen stations of the sun's daily course
（晨明， 朏明， 旦明， 蚤食， 晏食， 隅中， 正中， 小還， 餔時，
大還， 高舂， 下舂， 縣車， 黃昏， 定昏） and closes: 禹以為朝、
晝、昏、夜 — "Yu made of them morning, day, dusk, night"
(VERIFIED). **No station and no division carries a phase
assignment.** Together with WP1b's three schemes (Lingshu 44,
Suwen 22, the later zi-wu liu-zhu), the pattern is now consistent
across every source surveyed: times of day are richly schematised
and never phase-assigned in the early texts. GAP-3 stands as a
derivation gap, and the seed's dawn/midday/dusk/night phase table
remains correctly tagged as the project's own construction —
this survey strengthens that tag rather than disturbing it.

### 2.5 The eight winds (calendrical)

距日冬至四十五日條風至 — from winter solstice, a named wind
arrives every forty-five days: 條風， 明庶風， 清明風， 景風，
涼風， 閶闔風， 不周風， 廣莫風， each with a governmental action
(VERIFIED). The winds are keyed to the calendar cycle, not
assigned phases in the passage. *Dixing* carries a different,
directional eight-winds list (§4.5); the two lists share three
names and must be kept chapter-scoped.

### 2.6 Weather — the yin-yang axis only

> 天之偏氣，怒者為風；地之含氣，和者為雨。…陰陽相薄，感而
> 為雷，激而為霆，亂而為霧。陽氣勝則散而為雨露，陰氣勝則
> 凝而為霜雪。

Wind, rain, thunder, fog, dew, frost and snow are all explained
as states of yin-yang qi: rain is contained qi in harmony, or
yang prevailing and dispersing; frost and snow are yin prevailing
and congealing; fog is the two qi in disorder (VERIFIED). **No
weather phenomenon is assigned a phase anywhere in the surveyed
chapters.** Bearing on H6 (§7): the Han synthesis's own default
explanation of weather is mechanical and yin-yang; phase
assignments for weather were never going to be attestations.
This neither confirms nor refutes the project's H6 rain rule (a
derivation from the precipitation aspect); it confirms that the
rule's status as a derivation is the right status, and it is
positive evidence for the phase-null discipline on frost and fog,
whose textual explanations are congelation and disorder, not
phase membership.

## 3. *Shize* (ch. 5) — the monthly ordinances

The chapter follows the *Yueling* tradition month by month. Each
season's first month states the full row (VERIFIED, Sibu Congkan
text with Gao You's commentary):

| Row | Spring | Summer | Centre （季夏） | Autumn | Winter |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Position | 東方 | 南方 | 中央 | 西方 | 北方 |
| Stem-days | 甲乙 | 丙丁 | 戊己 | 庚辛 | 壬癸 |
| Phase in power | 盛德在木 | 盛德在火 | 盛德在土 | 盛德在金 | 盛德在水 |
| Creature class （蟲） | 鱗 scaled | 羽 feathered | 蠃 naked | 毛 furred | 介 shelled |
| Tone | 角 | 徵 | 宮 | 商 | 羽 |
| Number | 八 8 | 七 7 | 五 5 | 九 9 | 六 6 |
| Flavour | 酸 sour | 苦 bitter | 甘 sweet | 辛 pungent | 鹹 salty |
| Smell | 羶 rank | 焦 scorched | 香 fragrant | 腥 fishy | 腐 rotten |
| Sacrifice （祀） | 戶 door | 竈 hearth | 中霤 centre of the house | 門 gate | 井 well |
| First offered （祭先） | 脾 spleen | 胏 lungs | 心 heart | 肝 liver | 腎 kidneys |
| Food | 麥與羊 wheat & goat | 菽與雞 beans & chicken | 稷與牛 millet & cattle | 麻與犬 hemp & dog | 黍與彘 millet & pig |
| Weapon （兵） | 矛 spear | 戟 halberd | 劍 sword | 戈 dagger-axe | 鎩 long spear |

### 3.1 Against *Suwen* ch. 4

- **Numbers AGREE exactly** （八七五九六）. **Flavours AGREE.**
  **Smells agree except spring:** 羶 here (and in the *Yueling*)
  against ch. 4's 臊 — near-synonyms, both "rank"; record as a
  wording variant, not a scheme difference.
- **The animal row CONFLICTS, by transposition and substitution.**
  Ch. 4: chicken / goat / cattle / horse / pig. *Shize*: goat /
  chicken / cattle / dog / pig. Spring and summer are swapped;
  autumn's horse is replaced by the dog; centre and winter agree.
  Neither text is "wrong"; they are two schemes, and any consumer
  must cite which one it is using. (The *Yueling* agrees with
  *Shize* here — the medical and ritual traditions part on this
  row.)
- **The grain row CONFLICTS in three cells.** Ch. 4: wheat /
  millet / 稷 / rice / beans. *Shize*: wheat / beans / 稷 / hemp /
  millet. Summer, autumn and winter all differ.
- **New rows:** the sacrifice and its object, the weapon, and —
  the survey's most interesting single row — 祭先， the organ
  offered first.

### 3.2 The first-offered organ follows the controlling cycle

Gao You's commentary states the principle for spring outright:
春木勝土，言常食所勝也 — "spring's Wood conquers Earth; one
regularly eats what is conquered." The offered organ is the organ
of the phase the season's phase **controls**: Wood→Earth 脾
(spring ✓), Fire→Metal 胏 (summer ✓), Metal→Wood 肝 (autumn ✓).
The centre and winter break the pattern, and Gao You negotiates
both in his notes: for the centre's 心 (Fire's organ, where
Earth→Water would predict 腎） he offers 用所勝也 with the
alternative 一曰心土也，自用其藏也 ("one says: the heart counts
as Earth here; it uses its own organ"); for winter's 腎 he gives
腎水自開其藏也 ("the kidney, Water, opens its own store"). The
row is therefore recorded as: organised by the controlling cycle
in three of five cells, with the two exceptions visible already
to the chapter's own Han commentator. This is the earliest
ritual application of the controlling cycle as an organising
principle that the project has surveyed — evidence that the ke
cycle did organising work in the tradition, not only in medical
theory.

### 3.3 The centre's month

In *Shize* the centre is not seasonless: it occupies 季夏， the
last summer month, as its own block （其位中央…盛德在土）. This
is the *Yueling* arrangement and the second of the four Earth
placements now in the catalogue (ch. 4's 长夏； *Tianwen*'s 四季
ends; *Tianwen*'s 72-day reign; this centre month). The four are
four schemes, recorded side by side; the catalogue's standing
rule — cite the scheme with the source — applies to each.

## 4. *Dixing* (ch. 4) — forms of the earth

### 4.1 The controlling cycle, chained, and its dynamics

> 木勝土，土勝水，水勝火，火勝金，金勝木。

stated as a chain (VERIFIED) and immediately applied: 禾春生
秋死，菽夏生冬死，麥秋生夏死，薺冬生中夏死 — crops are born
in their phase's season and die in their controller's. The
chapter then gives a five-state dynamics for any phase in power
（壯）: taking Wood flourishing as the example — 木壯，水老
火生金囚土死 — Water (the mother) is 老 "aged", Fire (the
child) is 生 "being born", Metal (which Wood cannot control…
read as written: the phase Wood is controlled by) is 囚
"imprisoned", Earth (which Wood controls) is 死 "dead"; the full
set of five rotations is stated. The project's engine vocabulary
(propagation with restraint) has an attested Han cousin here: a
complete state vocabulary （壯/老/生/囚/死） over the two cycles.
Recorded as a scheme; adopting its vocabulary is a design
decision, not a survey finding.

The chapter also states a refining sequence — 煉土生木，煉木
生火，煉火生雲，煉雲生水，煉水反土 — in which Fire refines to
**cloud**, not Earth, and parallel refining sequences for
flavours （甘→酸→辛→苦→鹹→甘） and tones （宮→徵→商→羽→
角→宮）. Read in phase terms these sequences match neither
cycle; they are recorded as written, as their own scheme, and
not harmonised onto the cycles. The passage closes: 五行相治，
所以成器用 — "the five phases mutually govern; this is how
implements are made." Bearing on GAP-1: the tradition here
connects the phases to artefact-*making* as a process, while
assigning no artefact a phase — the same posture as the
*Kaogong Ji* (WP1b §6.4). GAP-1's shape is unchanged.

### 4.2 The five-region peoples — *Suwen* ch. 4's orifices corroborated

The chapter describes the peoples of the five regions (VERIFIED):

| | East | South | West | North | Centre |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Orifice | 竅通於目 eyes | 竅通於耳 ear | 竅通於鼻 nose | 竅通於陰 the lower orifice | 竅通於口 mouth |
| Tissue | 筋氣 sinews | 血脈 vessels | 皮革 skin | 骨幹 bone | 膚肉 flesh |
| Colour/organ | 蒼色主肝 liver | 赤色主心 heart | 白色主肺 lungs | 黑色主腎 kidneys | 黃色主**胃 stomach** |
| Grain suited | 麥 wheat | 稻 rice | 黍 millet | 菽 beans | 禾 grain |
| Fauna | 多虎豹 tigers & leopards | 多兕象 rhinos & elephants | 多旄犀 yaks & rhinos | 多犬馬 dogs & horses | 多牛羊 cattle & goats |

The orifice row **is *Suwen* ch. 4's row** — eyes, ear, nose,
the two lower orifices （陰）, mouth — including the two cells
that look eccentric against ch. 5 (heart–ear, kidney–lower
orifice). Ch. 4's scheme is therefore not a *Neijing* oddity:
it is the regional-body scheme, attested a generation or more
earlier in a non-medical text. The variant register (WP1 §1.6)
gains an entry: ch. 4's orifices, corroborated by *Huainanzi*
ch. 4; ch. 5's orifices remain the *Neijing*'s second scheme.

Two differences inside the corroboration, recorded as written:
the centre's organ is the **stomach （胃）**, not the spleen; and
the grain row differs from both *Suwen* ch. 4 and *Shize* (a
third grain scheme: wheat / rice / millet / beans / grain). The
fauna row is a different kind of row — characteristic animals of
a region, not sacrificial animals — and is not tallied against
the animal rows of §3.1.

### 4.3 Creature genealogies

The chapter gives each creature class a generation line
(VERIFIED; the human line's first link is OCR-damaged in the
fetched edition and is not relied on):

- Feathered: 羽嘉生飛龍，飛龍生鳳皇，鳳皇生鸞鳥，鸞鳥生庶鳥
  — "all feathered things are born of the common birds."
- Furred: 毛犢生應龍，應龍生建馬，建馬生麒麟，麒麟生庶獸.
- Scaled: 介鱗生蛟龍，蛟龍生鯤鯁，鯤鯁生建邪，建邪生庶魚 —
  the scaled line's ancestor is named 介鱗， "shelled-scaled",
  the two classes sharing a root here.
- Shelled: 介潭生先龍，先龍生玄黿，玄黿生靈龜，靈龜生庶龜.
- The damp line: a chain of moisture-generation （暖濕 "warm
  damp" and its descendants, partly OCR-damaged) producing the
  small creatures that arise from damp — the text's own home for
  soft, small, coveringless creatures. It is **not** the naked
  class.
- The human line runs separately and ends 聖人生庶人 — "the
  sage generates the common people."

And *Tianwen* adds a binary overlay (VERIFIED): 毛羽者，飛行
之類也，故屬於陽。介鱗者，蟄伏之類也，故屬於陰 — furred and
feathered fly, and belong to yang; shelled and scaled hibernate,
and belong to yin. No naked class appears in the binary.

### 4.4 Diet and land — two correspondence families the catalogue lacks

- Diet → disposition (VERIFIED): 食水者善游能寒，食土者無心
  而慧，食木者多力，食草者善走而愚，食葉者有絲而蛾，食肉者
  勇敢而悍，食氣者神明而壽，食谷者知慧而夭。不食者不死而神
  — what a creature eats fixes its capacities (water-eaters swim
  and endure cold; leaf-eaters make silk and become moths;
  grain-eaters are knowing and short-lived). Not phase-keyed;
  recorded as a correspondence family of a different kind.
- Land → people (VERIFIED): soils （堅土人剛，弱土人肥，壚土
  人大，沙土人細…), waters （白水宜玉，黑水宜砥，青水宜碧，
  赤水宜丹，黃水宜金）, and regional qi （山氣多男，澤氣多女，
  石氣多力…) each carry rows of effects. Environmental
  correspondence at family scale; noted, not itemised.

### 4.5 The second eight winds

何謂八風？東北曰炎風，東方曰條風，東南曰景風，南方曰巨風，
西南曰涼風，西方曰飂風，西北曰麗風，北方曰寒風 (VERIFIED) —
a directional list, different from *Tianwen*'s calendrical list
(§2.5) in five of eight names. The same chapter's generation
passage gives the *Tianwen*-named winds divine generators
（諸稽、攝提，條風之所生也…). One book, two wind schemes,
kept chapter-scoped like everything else in this survey.

## 5. *Jingshen* (ch. 7) — a different body

The chapter's body passage (verified at passage level, §0):

> 形體以成，五藏乃形。是故肺主目，腎主鼻，膽主口，肝主耳，
> 脾主舌。…天有風雨寒暑，人亦有取與喜怒。故膽為雲，肺為氣，
> 肝為風，腎為雨，脾為雷，以與天地相參也，而心為之主。

Two schemes, both incompatible with the *Neijing* tables:

- **Organ → orifice:** lungs→eyes, kidneys→nose, **gallbladder**
  →mouth, liver→**ear**, spleen→**tongue**. The five organs
  listed include the gallbladder and omit the heart (the heart is
  then named separately as ruler: 心為之主）. Against ch. 4
  (liver→eyes, heart→ear, spleen→mouth, lungs→nose, kidney→
  lower orifice) and ch. 5 (liver→eyes, heart→tongue, spleen→
  mouth, lungs→nose, kidney→ear), this is a third scheme sharing
  almost no cells with either. It is recorded as its own
  source-scoped scheme. It does not disturb the project's tables,
  which are *Neijing*-sourced; it does establish that organ–
  orifice assignment was not settled doctrine in the Han, which
  is worth the catalogue knowing.
- **Organ → weather:** gallbladder→clouds, lungs→qi/breath,
  liver→wind, kidneys→rain, spleen→thunder. (One modern
  transcription swaps wind and thunder between liver and spleen;
  the reading above follows the Gujin Tushu Jicheng quotation and
  the standard modern edition. The variant is recorded, not
  resolved by preference.) This is a body–weather correspondence
  of a kind the project does not hold: the organs *are* weather
  processes in the body. It assigns no phase to any weather
  phenomenon — consistent with §2.6.

**Emotions:** the passage names 取與 (taking and giving) and
喜怒 (joy and anger) as the human counterparts of wind, rain,
cold and heat. **No five-emotion table exists in the surveyed
chapters.** The project's emotion row （怒喜思憂恐 and its
variants) remains *Neijing*-attested only, now against one more
major source checked.

**Trigrams:** no trigram–phase assignment appears in the
surveyed chapters. GAP-4's staged history (WP1 §6.5) is
unaffected: the *Huainanzi* is not a source for the trigram
table, early or late.

## 6. The naked class — evidence on P2's open scope question

Part E registered P2 (CREATURE_CLASS_YUELING_V1) from the
*Yueling*: 鱗 Wood · 羽 Fire · 倮 Earth · 毛 Metal · 介 Water.
The 500-object run applied the naked class by resemblance to
seven soft-bodied creatures (octopus, squid, sea cucumber, toad,
frog, leech, silkworm), flagged T4, and left the scope question
open: does 倮 reach soft-bodied creatures generally, or only
skin-bearing ones? The evidence this survey assembled:

1. **The class's chief is the human, in every parallel scheme.**
   *Da Dai Liji · Zengzi Tianyuan*: 毛蟲之精者曰麟，羽蟲之精者
   曰鳳，介蟲之精者曰龜，鱗蟲之精者曰龍，裸蟲之精者曰聖人 —
   the essence of the naked creatures is **the sage**. The
   received commentary tradition on the *Yueling* (Chen Hao's
   note, transmitting the earlier commentators): 人為倮蟲之長
   — "the human is chief of the naked creatures." *Shize*'s own
   centre block writes the class 蠃， and Gao You's glosses treat
   the classes through their chiefs （鱗蟲龍為之長；毛蟲虎為
   之長；甲蟲龜為之長）.
2. **The one extension beyond humans is commentarial and is
   flagged as one man's reading.** The same note continues:
   《鄭氏》以為虎豹之屬 — "**Zheng [Xuan] held** [the naked
   class to include] tigers and leopards" (thin-furred, as Part E
   recorded). The tradition transmits this as Zheng's position,
   attributed and marked — not as the scheme's plain content.
3. **The *Huainanzi*'s own taxonomy gives soft creatures a
   different home.** *Dixing*'s genealogies (§4.3) run the human
   line separately （聖人生庶人） and derive the small damp-born
   creatures from the moisture chain. Nothing in the book places
   a worm, a silkworm, or any soft invertebrate among the naked.
   The silkworm appears in *Dixing* under diet （食葉者有絲而
   蛾 — leaf-eaters make silk), unclassed by covering.
4. **The other classes are defined by their exemplars in the
   commentaries** — scaled: 龍蛇之屬 "dragons and snakes"
   (Zheng Xuan), fish included (Gao You); shelled: the turtle as
   chief; furred: the tiger as chief (Gao You on *Shize*). The
   scheme as transmitted is a cosmology of chiefs, not a
   zoological key; its classes reach exactly as far as the
   texts and their commentators reach.

**Recommendation (for the user's ruling; nothing here changes
the registry by itself).** Rule the scope **narrow**: 倮 covers
humans by the scheme's plain content, and thin-furred beasts
only as Zheng Xuan's flagged commentarial extension (already the
run's T4-flagged treatment). The seven soft-bodied positions
should come down to T5 — no registered rule reaches them. The
frog and toad are the strongest resemblance cases (vertebrates
whose skin is genuinely bare), but resemblance is not
attestation, and P2's warrant is the *Yueling* scheme with its
commentary tradition; that warrant does not reach them. If the
project wants a soft-bodied rule, it should register one of its
own — a new P-rule with its own scope and falsification
condition — rather than stretch P2 past its evidence.

**Correction to this recommendation (2026-11-15, layered, not a
silent edit).** The recommendation above privileged the class's
exemplar over its criterion, and the user challenged it on two
points that hold: the soft creatures *are* bare-skinned by the
criterion the class is named for, and Zheng's thin-fur extension
— a tiger has a covering, however short — is the weaker
membership claim, not a bridge. The schemes' own formula (360
creatures per class, a chief over each) makes the chief the
foremost member, not the only one. The user then gave the
governing method: opinionated edge-case contradictions can
usually be avoided; alignment with the system and common sense
govern; the categories are Semblances （象） in the *Xici*'s
sense — things are figured by their material forms and their
character, and placed by according with the semblance. Under
that ruling the scope is settled the other way: **membership by
criterion — bare skin counts; the run's seven soft-bodied
positions stand as T4 predictions; Zheng's tiger/leopard
extension is set aside as an opinionated edge case, not a
precedent.** The survey's evidence sections stand as written;
only the recommendation is superseded, by this note.
