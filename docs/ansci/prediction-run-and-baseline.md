**Method discipline:** Part A was written from the cycles + the settled

## Part A — Predictions (written first)

**P1 — Chinese A-not-A questions exclude 吗 (ma).**
Derivation: interrogative force is a single clause-level modification
(R2). A-not-A realises that force by repeating the Wood node under its
own negation; 吗 realises the same force as a clause-final particle.
One force, two realisations competing for the same slot.
→ Prediction: 你去不去？(Nǐ qù bú qù? — "Are you going?") is grammatical,
and adding 吗 to an A-not-A question is ungrammatical.

**P2 — Chinese question words stay in place; English ones front.**
Derivation: a question word is an Earth node filling an ordinary slot;
interrogative force is clause-scope (Water/modality layer), which
requires no displacement of the Earth node. Displacement, if any, is a
connective-template choice of the language, not a meaning requirement.
→ Prediction: Chinese keeps the question word in its argument position
(你买什么？Nǐ mǎi shénme? — "What are you buying?", literally
"you buy what"), with no gap; English fronts it and leaves a gap (R3).
The meaning graphs are identical; only connective templates differ.

**P3 — Counting a Chinese noun forces a classifier, in fixed position.**
Derivation: number is a Metal category (the "cut"). In Chinese, a
Metal cut cannot reach an Earth node directly; the generating chain
Earth→Metal must be realised by a classifier — a Metal node the noun
itself generates (each noun class generates its own). Determiners/
numbers are also Metal-side.
→ Predictions: (a) *三书 is ungrammatical, 三本书 (sān běn shū —
"three [volume-classifier] book") is required; (b) the order is fixed
Number–Classifier–Noun; (c) a wrong classifier (one generated by a
different noun class) is a violation of the same kind as an agreement
error in English — an Earth→Metal generation mismatch.

**P4 — Serial verbs in Chinese chain without conjunctions, in event
order, and cannot be reordered freely.**
Derivation: multiple Wood nodes in one clause chain by the generating
cycle's closure (the state at the end of one Wood feeds the next Wood)
rather than by a Metal conjunction. Closure order is the event order.
→ Predictions: (a) 他去商店买东西。(Tā qù shāngdiàn mǎi dōngxi — "He
goes to the shop to buy things") needs no conjunction; (b) swapping
the verbs changes or destroys the meaning (buying precedes going is a
different, incoherent event chain); (c) an overt conjunction between
the verbs would change the structure from one chained event to two
coordinated ones.

**P5 — English relative-clause gaps are asymmetric: object gaps may be
silent, subject gaps may not.**
Derivation: zero realisation (settled connective shape) is licensed
where the missing element is recoverable from a controlling relation.
An object Earth is controlled by the verb's valency (Wood→Earth): an
empty object slot is announced by the verb itself. The subject Earth
is the clause's ground/anchor; a silent subject gap leaves two Wood
roots competing with no anchor telling the parser a clause boundary
exists.
→ Predictions: (a) "the book I bought" (object relative, pronoun
omitted) is grammatical; (b) *"the man came to dinner" intended as a
noun phrase meaning "the man who came to dinner" is ungrammatical —
the subject relative pronoun cannot be omitted.

**P6 — Chinese sentence-final particles stack in a fixed scope order.**
Derivation: clause-final particles are clause-scope attachments
(R7) — aspect 了 (le) innermost (it modifies the Wood event),
interrogative 吗 (ma) outside it (it modifies the whole proposition's
force), attitude particles outermost. Nested scopes must surface in
scope order, innermost first, because each outer particle takes the
already-modified clause as its input — the generating flow of the
clause applied repeatedly at the closure point.
→ Prediction: 了…吗 order occurs (你吃了吗？— "Have you eaten?"),
and the reverse order *吗…了 as stacked finals is ungrammatical.
