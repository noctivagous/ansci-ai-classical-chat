## 0. Method

**The ratified protocol, applied.** Classify *aspects* of an entity,
never the entity wholesale, on three axes recorded as separate
assertions — **material** (strongest attestation), **nature** (only
via a recorded property, source wording retained), **function**
(no automatic element; phase null until a functional correspondence
passes the prediction-admission test). Evidence levels: **A** —
directly attested material classification; **B** — attested
characteristic correspondence; **C** — project-derived (tagged
creative). Composites are classified by components. Abstention is
legitimate.

**Citation discipline.** Every assertion in this catalogue carries
its source text and chapter. **No citation, no assertion** — where
the surveyed texts are silent, the entry is marked **GAP**, never
filled from intuition. Where an item's exact placement is unsure,
it is marked **UNCERTAIN** rather than guessed. Items from later
materia medica and modern TCM textbooks are **not** imported into
these classical lists, however standard they later became.

**Survey scope.** Four anchors, read in standard transmitted
editions (consulted 2026-11-15): *Shangshu* 尚书 (Shàngshū) ·
*Hongfan* 洪范 (Hóngfàn, "Great Plan"); *Zuo Zhuan* 左传
(Zuǒ Zhuàn), Duke Xiang year 27; *Huangdi Neijing Lingshu* 灵枢
(Língshū) ch. 56, *Wuwei* 五味 (Wǔwèi, "Five Flavors"); *Huangdi
Neijing Suwen* 素问 (Sùwèn) ch. 4, *Jin Gui Zhen Yan Lun* 金匱真言論
(Jīn Guì Zhēn Yán Lùn) and ch. 5, *Yin Yang Ying Xiang Da Lun*
陰陽應象大論 (Yīn Yáng Yìng Xiàng Dà Lùn). Other chapters are out of
scope even where they contain parallel material; parallels are
noted as parallels, not surveyed.

**Cross-check verdicts** (§2): **AGREES** — a surveyed source states
the same assignment; **VARIANT** — a surveyed source states a
different assignment or scheme (both are recorded, source-scoped,
never flattened); **UNCITED** — the entry stands in the seed or the
curated table but no surveyed source states it (flagged, not
deleted).

Glosses for Chinese terms follow this chat's convention on first
use: character (pinyin, "meaning").

## 1. The anchor sources

### 1.1 *Shangshu · Hongfan* — the five processes and the five flavors

The first of the Nine Divisions is the Five Phases themselves:

> 五行：一曰水，二曰火，三曰木，四曰金，五曰土。水曰润下，火曰炎上，
> 木曰曲直，金曰从革，土爰稼穑。润下作咸，炎上作苦，曲直作酸，
> 从革作辛，稼穑作甘。
>
> "The Five Phases: first, Water; second, Fire; third, Wood;
> fourth, Metal; fifth, Earth. Water is called moistening and
> descending （润下， rùnxià); Fire, flaming and ascending （炎上，
> yánshàng); Wood, bending and straightening （曲直， qūzhí); Metal,
> following and transforming （从革， cónggé); Earth, sowing and
> harvesting （稼穑， jiàsè). Moistening-and-descending produces the
> salty; flaming-and-ascending the bitter; bending-and-straightening
> the sour; following-and-transforming the pungent; sowing-and-
> harvesting the sweet."

| Phase | Process (source wording) | Flavor produced |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Water | 润下 — moistening and descending | 咸 (xián, "salty") |
| Fire | 炎上 — flaming and ascending | 苦 (kǔ, "bitter") |
| Wood | 曲直 — bending and straightening | 酸 (suān, "sour") |
| Metal | 从革 — following and transforming | 辛 (xīn, "pungent") |
| Earth | 稼穑 — sowing and harvesting | 甘 (gān, "sweet") |

**What it supplies.** The characteristic *processes* of the five
phases — the root of every nature-axis classification (evidence
level B) — and the flavor row of the correspondence tables (level
B). **What it does not supply:** any classification of individual
objects. The passage defines what the phases *do*; it is not an
object taxonomy, and no object assignment may cite it directly
except through a recorded process/property.

### 1.2 *Zuo Zhuan*, Duke Xiang 27 — the five materials

Zihan 子罕 (Zǐhǎn) of Song, rejecting a reward request made on the
strength of the disarmament conference:

> 天生五材，民并用之，废一不可，谁能去兵？
>
> "Heaven produces the five materials （五材， wǔcái); the people
> use them all; not one may be discarded — who, then, could do
> away with weapons?"

**What it supplies.** The five *materials* as materials — the
direct historical warrant for the material axis (level A for the
material categories themselves: metal, wood, water, fire, earth as
stuffs people use). Two precisions: (i) the sentence names the five
materials collectively; their enumeration as 金、木、水、火、土
(metal, wood, water, fire, earth) is the received reading of the
commentary tradition, not a list in the sentence itself. (ii) The
five materials （五材） and the five phases （五行） are related but
are **not assumed to be identical taxonomies**: this passage speaks
of materials in human use; *Hongfan* speaks of processes. The
catalogue records them as related assertions from different
sources, per the protocol.

### 1.3 *Lingshu* ch. 56, *Wuwei* 五味 — the flavor lists

The chapter's respondent is Bogao 伯高 (Bógāo). Its organising
claim:

> 五味各走其所喜：谷味酸，先走肝；谷味苦，先走心；谷味甘，先走脾；
> 谷味辛，先走肺；谷味咸，先走肾。
>
> "Each of the five flavors goes to what it favours: sour first to
> the liver, bitter to the heart, sweet to the spleen, pungent to
> the lungs, salty to the kidneys."

The four food lists, **as the chapter gives them** — every item is a
**flavor correspondence (evidence level B)**, classifying the food
*by its flavor*, and explicitly **not** a whole-entity
classification of that food's substance, nature, or function:

| Flavor | Organ | 五谷 (grains) | 五果 (fruits) | 五畜 (livestock) | 五菜 (vegetables) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 酸 sour | liver | 麻 (má, "hemp/sesame" — UNCERTAIN, §4) | 李 (lǐ, "plum") | 犬 (quǎn, "dog") | 韭 (jiǔ, "chives") |
| 苦 bitter | heart | 麦 (mài, "wheat") | 杏 (xìng, "apricot") | 羊 (yáng, "goat/sheep") | 薤 (xiè, "rakkyo") |
| 甘 sweet | spleen | 秔米 (jīngmǐ, "rice") | 枣 (zǎo, "date") | 牛 (niú, "cattle") | 葵 (kuí, "mallow") |
| 辛 pungent | lungs | 黄黍 (huángshǔ, "yellow millet") | 桃 (táo, "peach") | 鸡 (jī, "chicken") | 葱 (cōng, "scallion") |
| 咸 salty | kidneys | 大豆 (dàdòu, "soybean") | 栗 (lì, "chestnut") | 猪 (zhū, "pig") | 藿 (huò, "bean leaves") |

Source lines: 五谷：秔米甘，麻酸，大豆咸，麦苦，黄黍辛。五果：枣甘，
李酸，栗咸，杏苦，桃辛。五畜：牛甘，犬酸，猪咸，羊苦，鸡辛。五菜：
葵甘，韭酸，藿咸，薤苦，葱辛。

The chapter also gives the five colors' flavor affinities
（五色：黄色宜甘，青色宜酸，黑色宜咸，赤色宜苦，白色宜辛）, the
**五宜** (Wǔyí, "five suitables") — the same food sets keyed to the
*diseased organ whose own flavor they carry* （脾病者，宜食秔米饭、
牛肉、枣、葵；心病者，宜食麦、羊肉、杏、薤；肾病者，宜食大豆黄卷、
猪肉、栗、藿；肝病者，宜食麻、犬肉、李、韭；肺病者，宜食黄黍、
鸡肉、桃、葱） — and the **五禁** (Wǔjìn, "five prohibitions":
肝病禁辛，心病禁咸，脾病禁酸，肾病禁甘，肺病禁苦）.

**A within-chapter variant, recorded not flattened.** The chapter's
closing passage re-keys the *same five food sets* by a complementary
rule （肝色青，宜食甘，秔米饭、牛肉、枣、葵皆甘。心色赤，宜食酸，
犬肉、麻、李、韭皆酸。脾色黄，宜食咸，大豆、猪肉、栗、藿皆咸。
肺色白，宜食苦，麦、羊肉、杏、薤皆苦。肾色黑，宜食辛，黄黍、鸡肉、
桃、葱皆辛）: the sets are constant, the organ each set is "suitable"
for rotates. The catalogue treats the flavor lists (the table above)
as the stable content and the two organ-keyings as source-scoped
variants of one chapter. (A near-identical complementary passage
stands in *Suwen* ch. 22 — outside this survey's scope, noted here
only as a parallel.)

**What it does not supply.** Any classification of these foods
beyond flavor: that cattle stand in the sweet set is a flavor
assertion about beef as food, not a claim that cattle are
Earth-natured animals. The pilot (§5) must not promote these rows.

### 1.4 *Suwen* ch. 4, *Jin Gui Zhen Yan Lun* — the five-direction scheme

The chapter's five passages （东方…南方…中央…西方…北方…) state one
scheme. Reconstructed as a table, every cell from the chapter text:

| | East | South | Centre | West | North |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Direction | 东方 | 南方 | 中央 | 西方 | 北方 |
| Color | 青 (qīng, "green/azure") | 赤 (chì, "red") | 黄 (huáng, "yellow") | 白 (bái, "white") | 黑 (hēi, "black") |
| Organ | 肝 liver | 心 heart | 脾 spleen | 肺 lungs | 肾 kidneys |
| Orifice | 目 eyes | **耳 ear** | 口 mouth | 鼻 nose | 二阴 the two lower orifices |
| Flavor | 酸 sour | 苦 bitter | 甘 sweet | 辛 pungent | 咸 salty |
| Class （类） | 草木 plants | 火 fire | 土 earth | 金 metal | 水 water |
| Animal | 鸡 chicken | 羊 goat/sheep | 牛 cattle | 马 horse | 彘 pig |
| Grain | 麦 wheat | 黍 millet | 稷 (jì, "millet" — gloss approximate, §4) | 稻 rice | 豆 beans |
| Planet | 岁星 Jupiter | 荧惑 Mars | 镇星 Saturn | 太白 Venus | 辰星 Mercury |
| Tone | 角 (jué) | 徵 (zhǐ) | 宮 (gōng) | 商 (shāng) | 羽 (yǔ) |
| Number | 八 8 | 七 7 | 五 5 | 九 9 | 六 6 |
| Tissue locus | 筋 sinews | 脉 vessels | 肉 flesh | 皮毛 skin & body hair | 骨 bone |
| Smell | 臊 (sāo, "rank") | 焦 (jiāo, "scorched") | 香 (xiāng, "fragrant") | 腥 (xīng, "fishy") | 腐 (fǔ, "rotten") |

East passage, verbatim: 东方青色，入通于肝，开窍于目，藏精于肝，
其病发惊骇，其味酸，其类草木，其畜鸡，其谷麦，其应四时，上为岁星，
是以春气在头也，其音角，其数八，是以知病之在筋也，其臭臊。

**Two further contents of the chapter.** (i) **Late summer belongs
to the centre**: the chapter's seasonal passages name 长夏
(chángxià, "late summer") as a season in its own right （春胜长夏，
长夏胜冬；长夏善病洞泄寒中） and key the centre to earth and the
spleen （中央为土，病在脾）. The five-direction table itself does not
assign the centre a season name — the assignment lives in the
seasonal passages of the same chapter. (ii) **The chapter divides
the day by yin-yang only** （平旦至日中，天之阳，阳中之阳也…合夜至
鸡鸣，天之阴，阴中之阴也…): a fourfold light division with **no
element assignments** — see GAP-3.

### 1.5 *Suwen* ch. 5, *Yin Yang Ying Xiang Da Lun* — the generation chains

Each direction is stated as a generation chain followed by a
parallel table. East, verbatim: 东方生风，风生木，木生酸，酸生肝，
肝生筋，筋生心，肝主目。…神在天为风，在地为木，在体为筋，在脏为肝，
在色为苍，在音为角，在声为呼，在变动为握，在窍为目，在味为酸，
在志为怒。怒伤肝，悲胜怒；风伤筋，燥胜风；酸伤筋，辛胜酸。

| | East | South | Centre | West | North |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Chain | 风→木→酸→肝→筋→心 | 热→火→苦→心→血→脾 | 湿→土→甘→脾→肉→肺 | 燥→金→辛→肺→皮毛→肾 | 寒→水→咸→肾→骨髓→肝 |
| Climate （在天） | 风 wind | 热 heat | 湿 damp | 燥 dryness | 寒 cold |
| Phase （在地） | 木 Wood | 火 Fire | 土 Earth | 金 Metal | 水 Water |
| Tissue （在体） | 筋 sinews | 脉 vessels | 肉 flesh | 皮毛 skin & hair | 骨 bone |
| Organ （在脏） | 肝 liver | 心 heart | 脾 spleen | 肺 lungs | 肾 kidneys |
| Color （在色） | 苍 (cāng, "dark green") | 赤 red | 黄 yellow | 白 white | 黑 black |
| Tone （在音） | 角 | 徵 | 宮 | 商 | 羽 |
| Voice （在声） | 呼 (hū, "shouting") | 笑 (xiào, "laughing") | 歌 (gē, "singing") | 哭 (kū, "weeping") | 呻 (shēn, "groaning") |
| Change （在变动） | 握 (wò, "grasping") | 忧 (yōu, "trembling speech"*) | 哕 (yuě, "retching") | 咳 (ké, "coughing") | 栗 (lì, "shivering") |
| Orifice （在窍） | 目 eyes | **舌 tongue** | 口 mouth | 鼻 nose | **耳 ear** |
| Flavor （在味） | 酸 sour | 苦 bitter | 甘 sweet | 辛 pungent | 咸 salty |
| Emotion （在志） | 怒 (nù, "anger") | 喜 (xǐ, "joy") | 思 (sī, "thought") | 忧 (yōu, "sorrow/worry") | 恐 (kǒng, "fear") |

*The south's 在变动为忧 uses 忧 in its older sense of halting,
trembling speech — a different word-in-context from the west's
在志为忧 (sorrow). The catalogue records both as written; glosses
must not merge them.

The chapter's general statement of the five qi (line: 人有五脏化五气，
以生喜怒悲忧恐） names **喜、怒、悲、忧、恐** — joy, anger, **grief
（悲， bēi)**, sorrow （忧）, fear — as the five emotions collectively,
without assigning them one per direction in that line. This matters
for the grief verdict in §2.1.

### 1.6 Where ch. 4 and ch. 5 differ — the variant register

The two chapters' schemes are **not identical**, and the catalogue
keeps both, source-scoped:

| Row | Suwen ch. 4 | Suwen ch. 5 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Heart's orifice | **耳 ear** | **舌 tongue** |
| Kidney's orifice | **二阴 two lower orifices** | **耳 ear** |
| East color word | 青 | 苍 |
| Emotions | no emotion row (disease-manifestation row instead: 惊骇 etc.) | full 在志 row: 怒喜思忧恐 |
| Animals, grains, planets, numbers, smells | stated | absent |
| Climates, voices, changes, generation chains | absent | stated |
| Centre's season | 长夏 named in seasonal passages | not named in the chains |

Any consumer of this catalogue must cite the chapter with the
scheme. A merged "Neijing table" that silently takes ch. 5's tongue
and ch. 4's planets is exactly the flattening the protocol forbids.

## 5. The 100-object pilot protocol

Ratified amendment: the ordinary-object sample is piloted at **100
objects — 20 each of tools, substances, materials, plants,
animals** — before scaling to 500.

**Object selection.** The 100 objects are listed and **frozen
before classification begins** (held-out discipline: no swapping in
easier objects mid-pilot). Selection aims at ordinary things a
person handles, eats, wears, or meets outdoors — the domains the
evaluations bottlenecked on — and deliberately includes composites
(tools with parts of different materials) and known hard cases from
§2.4 (chicken, rice, peach among the foods if foods are sampled as
substances/animals/plants — domain assignment of each object is
itself recorded).

**Per-object record** (fields per the ratified protocol, from v5
§1.4):

1. Identity and ordinary name.
2. Material composition (component-by-component for composites).
3. Characteristic nature (recorded properties, source wording kept).
4. Function (recorded; phase null by default).
5. For each property: directly **observed**, **sourced** (citation),
   or **inferred** (derivation shown).
6. Whether a traditional correspondence exists (citation or GAP).
7. Whether the classification is a project extension (level C).
8. Whether classification is genuinely **indeterminate** — recorded
   as abstention, with the reason.

Each assertion is keyed (concept, aspect) with aspect ∈ {material,
nature, function}, carries its evidence level (A/B/C) and provenance
separately, and cites this catalogue or marks GAP.

**Procedure.** (i) Write the assignment procedure down — this
catalogue plus the protocol — before touching the objects.
(ii) Two independent classification passes over the same frozen 100.
(iii) Disagreements adjudicated against the sources; where sources
are silent, the entry is GAP or level C with its derivation shown —
never settled by intuition or by majority vote. (iv) Coverage and
accuracy measured per the ratified threshold policy (Slice 1 bars:
coverage ≥ 90%, accuracy ≥ 90%, per-domain floor 85%, unsupported
assignments ≤ 5% — reported for the pilot as diagnostics against the
procedure, since the pilot's purpose is validating the procedure
itself).

**Success criterion.** The pilot succeeds if it produces a
**reproducible classification procedure**: a second pass, following
the written procedure without appeal to the first pass's answers,
reproduces the assignments within the ratified thresholds. A pilot
in which all 100 objects receive elements but no one else could
reproduce the assignments has failed; a pilot with honest
abstentions and a procedure that reproduces has succeeded.

### 6.2 Times of day — three schemes, three datasets (GAP-3)

- ***Lingshu* ch. 44, *Shun Qi Yi Ri Fen Wei Si Shi* 順氣一日分為四時.**
  "以一日分為四時，朝則為春，日中為夏，日入為秋，夜半為冬。" Four
  season-analogous points tied to a clinical rhythm (illnesses
  commonly clearer in the morning, quieter at midday, worse at
  dusk, severe at midnight) **with the chapter's own exception**:
  where a particular viscus governs the illness, its phase
  relationships can produce a contrary daily pattern. The
  exception is part of the source and is recorded with any entry
  drawn from this chapter. Licenses: a four-point daily analogy
  and that clinical account. Does not license: a twelve-slot daily
  phase table, a fixed element for every clock hour, or an
  invariant daily elemental cycle for every person.
- ***Suwen* ch. 22, *Zang Qi Fa Shi Lun* 藏氣法時論.**
  Organ-specific daily disease patterns （肝病者，平旦慧，下晡甚，
  夜半靜 — liver disease clearer at dawn, worse in the late
  afternoon, quiet at midnight; 心病者，日中慧，夜半甚，平旦靜；
  and corresponding patterns for spleen, lung, kidney).
  Disease-course observations by organ — not a general twelve-hour
  organ clock; the chapter's seasonal and five-phase
  correspondences of the viscera do not convert every time-of-day
  observation into an independent five-phase assignment.
- ***Lingshu* ch. 76, *Wei Qi Xing* 衛氣行.** A day has twelve
  辰； defensive qi circulates through yang by day and yin by
  night. A theory of defensive-qi circulation — **not** the later
  twelve-meridian organ clock, and not an assignment of channels
  to two-hour periods.
- ***Ziwu liuzhu* 子午流注 (later tradition).** The twelve
  double-hour channel assignments ("hour canons") were established
  by later generations from earlier records of qi circulation.

### 6.3 The *bencao* tradition — substances (GAP-2)

- ***Shennong Bencao Jing* 神農本草經 (Shénnóng Běncǎo Jīng,
  "Shennong's Materia Medica").** 365 medicinals in three grades
  (upper, middle, lower) concerning medicinal value, toxicity, and
  use — **not** five-phase membership. The surviving text is
  reconstructed through later transmission and quotation, so
  individual entries need textual witnesses and variant tracking.
  Example of the assertion type: 丹砂 (dānshā, "cinnabar") —
  "味甘、微寒" (sweet in flavor, slightly cold in nature), with
  medicinal applications and provenance. A nature/flavor
  assertion: it does not state that cinnabar is Fire because it is
  red, or Metal because it is a mineral.
- ***Bencao Jing Ji Zhu* 本草經集注 (Běncǎo Jīng Jí Zhù,
  "Collected Commentaries on the Materia Medica"), compiled by Tao

### 6.4 *Kaogong Ji* 考工記 (*Zhouli* tradition) — artefacts and making (GAP-1)

An attested artefact/making ontology — stronger evidence about
artefacts than a list of raw materials and trades:

> 知者創物。巧者述之守之，世謂之工。
> 爍金以爲刃，凝土以爲器，作車以行陸，作舟以行水。
> 天有時，地有氣，材有美，工有巧。合此四者，然後可以爲良。

"Those who know create things; the skilful transmit and preserve
them; these are called craftsmen." Metal is smelted to make
blades; earth is consolidated to make vessels; carts are made for
land travel; boats for water travel. Excellence in a made thing
depends on season, terrestrial conditions, good material, and
skilled workmanship. The text describes woodworking, metalworking,
leatherworking, colour-setting, polishing, and clay-working
crafts, and identifies products and specialist trades. It supplies
source evidence for the **structure** of an artefact ontology —
construction, material selection, craft skill, environmental
conditions, artefact quality — and for material/function
distinctions in the data model.

**What it does not supply:** a five-phase artefact taxonomy. It
assigns no blade, vessel, cart, or boat to Wood, Fire, Earth,
Metal, or Water, and "a boat travels on water" does not establish
Water as the phase of that function (see GAP-6). Elemental
artefact-level assignment remains derivation — hypothesis **H5**
in `repair-programme.md`. (Kept distinct, per the v6 survey, from
the *Zuo Zhuan* Xiang 27 attestation of the five materials in
human use, §1.2: early sources discuss materials and making
without turning those discussions into a five-phase artefact
taxonomy.)

**Verification (2026-11-15) — VERIFIED.** Editions fetched: the
complete text at 漢典古籍 (*Zhouli · Dong Guan Kao Gong Ji* 第六，
經部·十三經） — read in full; the *Zhouli Zhushu* commentary
edition (Shidianguji, search-indexed text) corroborating; ctext,
*Rites of Zhou: Dong Guan Kao Gong Ji* (search-indexed excerpts;
direct opening blocked as elsewhere). All three quotations are
exact as transmitted: 知者創物，巧者述之守之，世謂之工 (several
popular web reprints corrupt 知者 to 知得 — not the reading of
either edition fetched); **爍**金以為刃 (the graph is 爍， not 鑠）,
凝土以為器，作車以行陸，作舟**以**行水 (some reprints drop the 以）;
天有時，地有氣，材有美，工有巧，合此四者，然後可以為良. **Scope
actually read: the entire transmitted text** — the general
preface and every craft section （輪人、輿人、輈人，筑氏、冶氏、桃氏、
鳧氏、栗氏、段氏，函人、鮑人、韗人，畫繢、鍾氏、㡛氏，玉人、磬氏、矢人、
陶人、旊人，梓人、廬人、匠人、車人、弓人， with the lost sections
noted as lost). **The claim under test holds in that full scope:
no passage assigns an individual artefact — blade, vessel, cart,
boat, bell, measure, bow — to one of the five phases; the string
五行 does not occur in the text.** Five-phase-adjacent material
found, recorded as adjacent only: the 六齊 (six alloy ratios,
differentiating artefact types by tin proportion, with no phase
names); the 畫繢 colour-direction scheme （東方謂之青，南方謂之赤，
西方謂之白，北方謂之黑，天謂之玄，地謂之黃…雜四時五色之位） — colours
and directions, not artefact assignments; the 輈人 cosmic
symbolism of cart parts （軫之方也，以象地也；蓋之圜也，以象天也…);
and the preface's 以飭五材 (ordering the five materials) — the
materials collectively, again not an artefact taxonomy.

**Eliminated candidates.** The *gua qi* 卦氣 tradition and the
twelve sovereign hexagrams （十二辟卦） are calendrical
hexagram-to-season/month schemes; a hexagram assigned to a month
is not thereby assigned a trigram phase, and neither supplies the
table. The *Yi Wei* apocrypha passage cited in the scholarship
（乾鑿度： Taiji → Heaven/Earth → four seasons → eight trigrams)
concerns generation order, not phase allocation. These are
recorded as **non-sources for the table**, with reasons — the
confirmed-absence discipline applies to candidates as well as to
texts.

**Catalogue wording (adopted 2026-11-15).**

> The *Shuo Gua* does not state the complete trigram–five-phase
> table. It supplies trigram images, directions and seasonal
> correspondences, but not a complete five-phase assignment. The
> received *Jing Shi Yi Zhuan*, traditionally attributed to Jing
> Fang, contains dispersed evidence for the standard eight-trigram
> allocation — more direct than a purely contextual reading
> suggests, since the eight-palace openings state the assignments
> for the palaces headed by each pure trigram. Wei Xiang's Western
> Han memorial provides an earlier but incomplete directional
> scheme. A concise complete statement is found in Kong Yingda's
> Tang *Zhouyi Zhengyi*, with a further explicit rationale in Su
> Shi's Song *Dongpo Yizhuan*. Warring States Tsinghua *Shi Fa*
> material preserves a different and partly disputed
> trigram–phase arrangement. The standard table is therefore an
> historically developed synthesis, not a direct statement of the
> *Shuo Gua*.

**Storage rule (unchanged from §6.1).** The standard table is
stored under the `later_phase_assignment` correspondence kind,
sourced to this reception strand — never as a *Shuo Gua*
correspondence, and never collapsed into a single
`trigram_element` field.
