## Reading

The provider's gain is lexical, exactly as designed: sentences whose only obstacle was a verb or noun outside the hand lexicon now parse, and their content words classify through the table or the WordNet climb. What the provider does NOT fix is construction coverage: questions with auxiliaries and embeddings the template parser has no shape for, multi-clause sentences, and phrasings whose verbs it cannot slot still fail, with the same thrown reasons as before. Exact round-trip remains the stricter bar — a sentence can parse and classify well yet generate back in a canonicalised form (articles, progressive aspect, and word order are the usual losses). Regressions, where a derived entry changes a parse the hand lexicon already handled, are listed above in full.

One aggregate needs its denominator read aloud: mean classifications per parsed sentence FELL (0.64 → 0.41), because the ON arm parses 43 additional sentences whose everyday vocabulary mostly has no classification to earn. On the 25 sentences both arms parse, mean classifications went 0.64 → 0.60 — the provider adds classifications where it can and dilutes the average only by widening the parsed set.

And a caveat about the flipped list: parse success is not semantic adequacy. Several flipped sentences are the old system's ABSTENTION probes ("What is my neighbour's phone number?", "Who won the football match last night?") — questions a chat should decline for lack of testimony. This run measures only whether the sentence layer can take them apart and what they classify to; whether anything should ANSWER them is a different layer's question, and nothing here answers it.
