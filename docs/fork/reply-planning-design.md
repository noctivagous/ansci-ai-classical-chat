# Reply Planning from the Ontology — design note

Status: DESIGN NOTE (2026-11-15). Commissioned by the user as a
short note for judgment before anything is built. Nothing in
this note is implemented. It covers parts 1 and 2 of the Path to
a Working Chatbot (dialogue core; reply generation). The user's
decisions on §7 questions 1, 3 and 4 were recorded the same day
(§7); question 2 remains open.

## 1. The gap

The turn loop (`src/fork/turn.ts`) already does three of the
four things a conversation needs. It parses a sentence into a
meaning graph; it retains the semantics in a session (entities,
events, states, carried across turns); and it derives
conclusions — derivation paths over the correspondence graph,
cycle paths between the turn's states and the session's. What
it does not do is speak from those conclusions. Its replies are
a small hand-written wording set, steered by the mechanism but
not generated by it. The generator (`src/fork/generate.ts`) can
turn any meaning graph back into English or Chinese — measured
at 97.7–98.2% feature preservation on round trips — but nothing
builds it a *reply* graph. It grades round-trips; it is not the
reply surface.

The missing step is reply planning: take the turn's
conclusions, build a meaning graph for the reply, hand that
graph to the generator. The naive way to build it — a planner
module with a dialogue-act taxonomy and template assembly —
would break two standing rules at once: the graph-distance rule
(a reply is never a direct graph query pasted out; every turn
travels through the whole architecture) and Part F (try the
ontology first; an engineering fallback is reported as an
ontology failure). So the planning step itself has to be made
out of the ontology. This note is how.

## 2. Three operations, all already prototyped somewhere

### 2.1 What to say: the move is a cycle relation

The steering probe already selects one move per turn, and its
selection is not a dialogue act — it is whichever relation
holds in the graph. The new state's element generates toward
the next content: follow it, toward expression. A state
controls an earlier state: address the restrainer, to release
what it restrains. A state has been vented: meet it from the
restraining side. The probe makes that one choice and hands it
to hand-written wording. The design generalises the same
selection to composing the whole reply: candidate contents are
the turn's derivations, and where rivals compete, the
arbitration is the sense machinery's — coherence support from
the surrounding graph (the pattern that already chooses between
rival readings of a word, in `src/fork/sense.ts`). No act
taxonomy is introduced. If a conversational move has no cycle
relation or derivation behind it, the planner does not invent
one; see §4.

### 2.2 How strongly to say it: support becomes qualification

Derivations already carry priced support — the sense layer's
weights: same element 1.0, a generating relation 0.8, a
controlling relation 0.4. Under this design, the support of the
derivation a reply rests on is written into the reply graph
itself, as the qualification states the generator already knows
how to realise — the modality and aspect states of the stage-5
machinery. A strongly supported conclusion is built as a plain
predication. A weakly supported one is built qualified, so the
hedge is in the meaning graph, checkable in the trace, rather
than pasted into the wording. And when no derivation reaches
the floor, the computation's output is the decline — which the
turn loop already produces, naming what is missing. Abstention
stops being a policy layered over the planner and becomes a
result of the same arithmetic as everything else.

### 2.3 Building the reply graph: a graph-to-graph transform

A derivation path is already a chain of typed relations. The
winter derivation — winter, ELEMENT_OF Water, POLARITY_OF Yin,
HAS_QUALITY cold — becomes a meaning graph mechanically: the
topic entity as subject, the derived quality as its predicated
state, and the path itself available as subordinate causal
material. That is the shape of the probe's one fully-derived
answer today ("Winter is cold, because it is the season turned
most fully toward cold") — except that today the sentence is
hand-worded, and under this design it is generated, from a
graph the planner built out of the derivation. The reply graph
is an ordinary meaning graph of the parser's own record type,
so the checker, the feature machinery and the round-trip
measurements all apply to replies unchanged.

Questions are the same transform run on a gap. A genuine gap —
the only kind the architecture permits a question about — is a
graph fact, in one of two shapes: a role the verb's valency
requires that the situation does not fill (the stage-1 valency
machinery already computes these), or a node whose
classification is split between two rival derivations with
comparable support (finite ambiguity, in the sealed protocol's
sense). Asking is generating a question graph whose missing
piece is exactly that gap. A question with no gap behind it is
not asked; a gap the person cannot fill is not asked about
either — both halves of the standing rule fall out of the
representation.

## 3. Worked shapes

- **Winter.** Asked why winter is cold, the planner takes the
  derivation path, builds {winter — predicated state: cold}
  with the path as its causal subordinate, at plain-assertion
  strength (the path is short and same-direction). The
  generator realises it. The trace shows the path, the support,
  and the graph — the same three things the probe's trace shows
  today, with the graph now the reply's actual source.
- **Anger, three turns.** "I heard the news" records an event
  and no state; with nothing for the cycles to steer by, the
  reply takes up the event and asks after its content — the gap
  is the event's unfilled content role. "I was furious" adds a
  Wood state; the generating direction selects expression, and
  the reply graph predicates the person's fury against the
  recorded event and asks for its venting. "I shouted at him"
  vents it; the controlling side selects settling. The probe
  makes these three choices today; the design changes only
  where the words come from.

## 4. What stays engineering — and what gets recorded

Word order and surface realisation stay in the connective
layer, where the two-layer design already puts them: generation
is meaning graph plus language precedence templates, and the
templates carry no knowledge claims. The ontology decides the
content, the stance and the strength; it does not conjugate
verbs.

Social formulae — greetings, leave-takings, pure politeness —
have no derivation behind them and make no knowledge claim.
They are the likeliest place for the build to reach for a
template. Under Part F that reach is recorded as an ontology
gap in the build report, named as one, rather than passing as
a quiet exception; whether a thin conventional layer is
admitted for them, labelled as conventional, is the user's
call at that point, not the build's.

## 5. Pieces: what exists, what is new

- Exists: `turn.ts` (the probe — v0 of move selection, one
  choice per turn, hand wording); `session.ts` (the situation
  record the planner reads); `join.ts` `derive()` (derivation
  paths); `sense.ts` (support weights and the arbitration
  pattern); `generate.ts` (realisation, both languages);
  the stage-5 node states (qualification the generator can
  already realise).
- New: one transform — situation plus derivations in, reply
  meaning graph out (working name `plan.ts`). No new node or
  edge vocabulary is proposed. If the build finds the reply
  graph needs a relation the meaning graph does not have, that
  is a finding to record and bring back, not a silent
  extension.

## 6. Acceptance

- Fresh transcripts are the gate, as they already are for the
  turn loop: new scenarios, judged turn by turn, never the ones
  that exposed a failure.
- Every reply's trace names the derivation it rests on, its
  support, and the reply graph built from it. A reply whose
  trace cannot name those three does not count, however good it
  reads.
- The surface ban extends to generated replies unchanged: no
  element, polarity or cycle vocabulary on the surface (the
  existing leak scan in `test/fork-turn.test.ts` runs over the
  new surface too).
- A decline must cite its ground: the support shortfall, or the
  missing role. "I don't know" without a named ground is a
  planner failure, recorded as one.
- The four scripted scenarios keep passing. Their traces keep
  the same structure; only the wording's origin changes.

## 7. Decisions (user, 2026-11-15) and the one open question

1. **Composition order — decided: build both, support is the
   default.** When several conclusions compose into one reply,
   both orderings are built out so they can be seen side by
   side: ordered by support, and ordered by relation to the
   newest state. Support-order is the default; relation-order
   is the comparison that can displace it on evidence.
2. **The causal subordinate — OPEN.** When the planner builds a
   reply from a derivation, the path can be realised as a
   subordinate "because" clause, or left in the trace. Two
   policies are on the table. (a) Demand-driven: the path is
   verbalised only when the person asks why, or challenges the
   conclusion; otherwise the reply is the bare conclusion.
   (b) Support-driven: the path is verbalised whenever the
   derivation's support is middling — the reply offers its own
   evidence exactly when the conclusion is not strong enough
   to stand naked; strong conclusions stand alone. Under (b),
   §2.2's dial turns one notch further: support controls not
   only qualification but evidence-offering. A "why" question
   triggers the clause under either policy. Undecided.
3. **Language — decided: English-first, transform
   language-neutral.** The planner lands English-first (the
   turn loop's current reach); the transform itself is built
   language-neutral, so Chinese reply graphs follow from the
   same machinery when the turn loop's Chinese side is built,
   rather than the planner being rebuilt for them.
4. **Disposition — decided, broader than this note's
   proposal.** The note proposed disposition as a tie-break
   bias at §2.1's arbitration and nowhere else. The user's
   ruling supersedes that: disposition is itself composed of a
   Five Element combination, and it influences the system's
   processes generally — arbitration, steering, generation —
   so that a disposition of one composition actually produces
   different responses from a disposition of another, by
   changing how the processes run, not by supplying content.
   The same composition treatment is available to other
   architectural aspects: an aspect can be parameterised by an
   elemental composition and thereby change behaviour across
   the system. Two standing limits hold unchanged: disposition
   is architecture, never surface (no reply names it), and it
   biases processing only — it is never a source of testimony.
