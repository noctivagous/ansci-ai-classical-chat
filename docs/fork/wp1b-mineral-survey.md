# WP1b — Bounded mineral survey: the 玉石 (jade-and-stones) substances

Date: 2026-11-15. Status: **complete as scoped** — 50 entries
surveyed under the field discipline of `wp1-classification-survey.md`
§6.3. This is a survey of the source tradition's classification
practice, **not** a project classification exercise: **no element
is assigned to any substance anywhere in this document.**

---

## 0. Scope, sources, method

**Scope.** The mineral/stone （玉石， yùshí, "jade and stones")
substances of the *Shennong Bencao Jing* 神農本草經 as transmitted
in the *Bencao Jing Ji Zhu* 本草經集注 tradition — i.e. the
*Benjing* 本經 stratum plus the *Mingyi Bielu* 名醫別錄 stratum
that Tao Hongjing's compilation merged with it — as that merged
tradition is preserved in the *Zhenglei Bencao* 證類本草.

**The count, honestly.** The *Benjing* mineral set is **46 items
under the old count**: 18 upper + 16 middle + 12 lower. Sun
Xingyan's 孫星衍 reconstruction merges several of them （鐵落 and
鐵 into 鐵精； 粉錫 with 錫鏡鼻； the three salts into one entry;
and counts 五色石脂 as one headword), giving **41 entries**. This
survey keeps the old count of 46 as its *Benjing* rows (merged
items get their own rows, sharing the merged entry text, marked
as such) and adds **4 *Bielu* additions** with fetched entry
texts — 玉屑， 綠青， 芒消 (upper), 金屑 (middle) — for **50 entries
in total**. Further *Bielu* mineral headwords identified but
**not** surveyed （銀屑， 玄石， 石腦， 玉英 in the middle grade;
伏龍肝 as a lower-grade candidate) are recorded as reserves in
§5, not silently dropped.

**Witnesses used (all fetched 2026-11-15).**
- **Layer S** — Sun Xingyan's reconstruction of the *Benjing*
  alone (short entries; the *Benjing* stratum): guoxueku text of
  Sun's edition, vols. 1–3; the Taiyi transcription of the same;
  the jicheng.tw text (CM098). Cited below as "Sun".
- **Layer Z** — the *Zhenglei Bencao* transmitted text
  (Shidianguji SK1441, vols. 3–5, cross-checked on Wikisource):
  *Benjing* + *Bielu* merged into expanded head lines, usually
  with an explicit toxicity word. Cited as "Zhenglei".
- **Qianjin Yifang** 千金翼方 vol. 2 (Wikisource) and **Xin Xiu
  Bencao** 新修本草 (theqi.com text) — for the *Bielu*-stratum
  entries and cross-checks.
- ctext's *Shennong Bencao Jing* could not be opened directly
  (anti-scraping interstitial); its search-indexed text is used
  **only** for recension-variant notes, and is labelled as such
  wherever used.

**Field discipline (per §6.3).** Each entry records: original
name (characters + pinyin); stratum and grade; flavor and thermal
nature **in the source's wording**; toxicity **as stated or
recorded as not stated** — never inferred from grade; indications
as stated (condensed, in the text's own terms); modern material
identification, marked as editorial and flagged † where
contested; and the explicit five-phase statement — quoted if
present, otherwise **RECORDED ABSENT**. A field that could not be
sourced is marked NOT SOURCED / NOT FETCHED rather than filled.

**Two layers, two answers.** Layer S and Layer Z systematically
differ (Zhenglei head lines merge *Bielu* material into the
*Benjing* line: flavors expand, toxicity words appear,
indications lengthen). Both are recorded; neither is flattened
into the other. Where the layers conflict, the table shows both.

## 6. Findings — what the tradition's classification practice actually looks like

1. **No substance is assigned a phase. 0 of 50.** Not one entry
   in the surveyed set — *Benjing* or *Bielu*, any grade — states
   a five-phase membership for its substance. The single
   correspondence clause in the whole set is 五色石脂's "each,
   following its colour, supplements one of the five viscera"
   (entry 16): a colour→viscus correspondence inside an
   indication, not a classification of the substance. Whatever
   the project's ontology does with these substances, it cannot
   cite this tradition as having classified them by phase.
2. **The classificatory work is done by flavor, thermal nature,
   indications, and grade.** 46 of the 50 entries state a flavor
   and a thermal nature in their primary text; one states nature
   only （鐵精， "平" with no flavor word); three state neither in
   their *Benjing* main clause （鐵，戎鹽，大鹽 — all three are
   clauses of merged or minimal entries). Flavor+nature is the
   tradition's substance-level descriptive axis; the *Neijing*
   correspondence passages (§1 of the main catalogue) are a
   separate evidence path and are not restated here.
3. **Toxicity is not a *Benjing* entry-level field at all.**
   In **0 of the 46** *Benjing* items does the entry itself state
   有毒 or 無毒. Toxicity lives in the grade frame （上品 classed
   無毒 as a class, 中品 mixed, 下品 多毒 — class-level rules in
   the preface) and becomes entry-level only in the transmitted
   layer: an explicit toxicity value is available for 41 of the
   50 surveyed entries, **in every case from the *Bielu*/
   transmitted layer** (Zhenglei head lines or the *Bielu*
   entries themselves), never from the *Benjing* stratum. A
   survey that reported "toxicity" for these substances without
   naming the layer would be manufacturing a field the base text
   does not have.
4. **The transmitted layer systematically expands the base
   text.** Zhenglei head lines lengthen flavor strings （消石
   苦寒 → 苦辛寒大寒； 朴消 likewise; 石膽 酸寒 → 酸辛寒 and acquires
   有毒）, add toxicity words, and lengthen indications with
   *Bielu* material. The two layers give different answers to
   the same field; both are recorded above because flattening
   them is exactly the error the catalogue's citation discipline
   exists to prevent.
5. **The 玉石 category is a materia-medica shelf, not a
   mineralogy.** The surveyed set contains plant ash （冬灰）,
   forge products （鐵，鐵落，鐵精）, manufactured compounds and
   preparations （鉛丹，粉錫，石灰，金屑，玉屑）, salts of several
   kinds, a metal （水銀 is an element; 鐵 a worked metal), and
   one entry （玉泉） whose material the tradition itself could
   not fix. "Mineral/stone" is the category's name, not its
   membership criterion; the membership criterion is medicinal
   use of stone-like and earth-derived substances.
6. **Counting is recension-relative, at every level.** *Benjing*
   minerals: 46 items (old count) = 41 entries (Sun's merges);
   五色石脂 is 1 headword or 6 entries (collective + five
   *Bielu*-line splits); the *Zhenglei* lower-grade *Benjing*
   count is 12 in one edition, 11 in another; grade placement
   itself varies （水銀 and 石膽 sit in different grades in the
   ctext-transmitted *Shennong* text than in Sun/Zhenglei);
   headwords vary （涅石/礬石； 太乙/太一餘糧； 鹵鹽/鹵鹹）. Any
   downstream use of this survey must cite counts with their
   recension attached.

## 7. Abstentions and limitations

- **Glass.** No glass entry appears in the surveyed strata, and
  none was surveyed. （玻璃 does occur in the *Zhenglei* upper
  list — among the 陳藏器-era additions, outside this survey's
  *Benjing*+*Bielu* scope; it is recorded here only to say it was
  seen and deliberately not surveyed.) The standing abstention
  of §6.3 is unchanged: glass's phase classification remains
  **unassigned**.
- **Modern identifications are editorial.** They are the
  standard identifications of the materia medica literature,
  supplied for orientation; no identification is a source
  assertion, and no modern-identification reference work was
  systematically surveyed for this document. The † flags rest
  largely on disputes recorded **inside the fetched pre-modern
  texts themselves** （膚青 unrecognised by Tao's time; 青琅玕's
  competing glosses; the 孔公孽/殷孽 and 玄石/慈石 debates;
  玉泉's unfixed material). Unflagged identifications are the
  uncontroversial ones (cinnabar, mica, talc, gypsum, hematite,
  mercury, realgar, orpiment, sulfur…), not verified claims.
- **Fields not fetched, recorded rather than filled:**
  太乙餘糧's separate Zhenglei head line (entry 13); 錫鏡鼻's
  Zhenglei flavor/toxicity (entry 43); 芒消's toxicity in the
  Qianjin text (entry 21). The *Bielu* attributions of entries
  19–21 and 38 rest on volume-header counts, list arithmetic,
  and Tao's 芒消 note — not on the white/black typography, which
  plain-text editions lose. One fetch failed outright (Wikisource
  *Mingyi Bielu* vol. 1, HTTP 403) and was not retried; no entry
  above depends on it.
- **ctext** was reachable only through its search index
  (anti-scraping interstitial on direct opening). Everything
  resting on ctext alone is labelled "search-indexed text only"
  and is used only for variant notes, never as a table's primary
  witness.
